# The Web Conference (WWW) 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [The Web Conference (WWW) 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://dl.acm.org/doi/proceedings/10.1145/3589334) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [Message Injection Attack on Rumor Detection under the Black-Box Evasion Setting Using Large Language Model](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3648139)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Message Injection Attack on Rumor Detection under the Black-Box Evasion Setting Using Large Language Model” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel black-box attack method, HMIA-LLM, against these rumor detectors, which uses the Large Language Model to generate malicious messages and inject them into the targeted MPTs.
- [Explainable Fake News Detection with Large Language Model via Defense Among Competing Wisdom](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645471)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Explainable Fake News Detection with Large Language Model via Defense Among Competing Wisdom” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To detect fake news from a sea of diverse, crowded and even competing narratives, in this paper, the authors propose a novel defense-based explainable fake news detection framework.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Poisoning Attack on Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645422)
  - **Research question:** How does “Poisoning Attack on Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The study is the first work to systematize the risks of FKGE poisoning attacks, from which the authors develop a novel framework for poisoning attacks that force the victim client to predict specific false facts.
- [Fake Resume Attacks: Data Poisoning on Online Job Platforms](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645524)
  - **Research question:** How does “Fake Resume Attacks: Data Poisoning on Online Job Platforms” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors develop an end-to-end "fake resume" generation framework, titled FRANCIS, that induces systematic prediction errors via data poisoning.
- [Poisoning Federated Recommender Systems with Fake Users](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645492)
  - **Research question:** How does “Poisoning Federated Recommender Systems with Fake Users” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a novel fake user based poisoning attack named PoisonFRS to promote the attacker-chosen targeted item in federated recommender systems without requiring knowledge about user-item rating data, user attributes, or the aggregation rule used by the server.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [FedDSE: Distribution-aware Sub-model Extraction for Federated Learning over Resource-constrained Devices](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645416)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “FedDSE: Distribution-aware Sub-model Extraction for Federated Learning over Resource-constrained Devices” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Motivated by this finding, the authors propose a novel method called FedDSE, which can reduce the conflicts among clients by extracting sub-models based on the data distribution of each client.
- [Privacy-Preserving and Fairness-Aware Federated Learning for Critical Infrastructure Protection and Resilience](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645545)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Privacy-Preserving and Fairness-Aware Federated Learning for Critical Infrastructure Protection and Resilience” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose confined gradient descent (CGD) that enhances the privacy of federated learning by eliminating the sharing of global model parameters.
- [Incentive and Dynamic Client Selection for Federated Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645462)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Incentive and Dynamic Client Selection for Federated Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** At the upper level, the authors integrate deep reinforcement learning into federated unlearning to dynamically select remaining clients to join the unlearning process to mitigate the bias introduced by the unbalanced data distribution among clients.
- [DPAR: Decoupled Graph Neural Networks with Node-Level Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645531)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “DPAR: Decoupled Graph Neural Networks with Node-Level Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a Decoupled GNN with Differentially Private Approximate Personalized PageRank (DPAR) for training GNNs with an enhanced privacy-utility tradeoff.
- [Federated Learning Vulnerabilities: Privacy Attacks with Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645514)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Federated Learning Vulnerabilities: Privacy Attacks with Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the Gradient Guided Diffusion Model (GGDM), a novel learning-free approach based on a pre-trained unconditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), aimed at improving the effectiveness and reducing the difficulty of implementing gradient based privacy attacks on complex networks and high-resolution images.
- [Breaking the Trilemma of Privacy, Utility, and Efficiency via Controllable Machine Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645669)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Breaking the Trilemma of Privacy, Utility, and Efficiency via Controllable Machine Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address the challenges, the authors present Controllable Machine Unlearning (ConMU), a novel framework designed to facilitate the calibration of MU.
- [Federated Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network for Privacy-preserving Recommendation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645693)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Federated Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network for Privacy-preserving Recommendation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Following this setting, the authors propose a federated heterogeneous graph neural network (FedHGNN) based framework, which facilitates collaborative training of a recommendation model using distributed HINs while protecting user privacy.
- [Towards Personalized Privacy: User-Governed Data Contribution for Federated Recommendation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645690)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Towards Personalized Privacy: User-Governed Data Contribution for Federated Recommendation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, this paper presents a cloud-device collaborative graph neural network federated recommendation model, named CDCGNNFed.
- [Cardinality Counting in "Alcatraz": A Privacy-aware Federated Learning Approach](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645655)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Cardinality Counting in "Alcatraz": A Privacy-aware Federated Learning Approach” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce an innovative privacy-centric solution for the cardinality counting dilemma, leveraging a federated learning framework.

## 4. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [Divide, Conquer, and Coalesce: Meta Parallel Graph Neural Network for IoT Intrusion Detection at Scale](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645457)
  - **Research question:** How does “Divide, Conquer, and Coalesce: Meta Parallel Graph Neural Network for IoT Intrusion Detection at Scale” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose an offline reinforcement learning algorithm, called Graph-Embedded Adversarially Trained Actor-Critic (G-ATAC), to learn a coalition formation policy that jointly optimizes intrusion detection accuracy, communication overheads, and computational complexities of coalitional NIDSs.

## 5. Other AI Security and Privacy

- [Fingerprinting the Shadows: Unmasking Malicious Servers with Machine Learning-Powered TLS Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645719)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Fingerprinting the Shadows: Unmasking Malicious Servers with Machine Learning-Powered TLS Analysis” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors examine state-of-the-art fingerprinting techniques and extend a machine learning pipeline for effective and practical server classification.
