# The Web Conference (WWW) 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [The Web Conference (WWW) 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://dl.acm.org/doi/proceedings/10.1145/3543507) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Training-free Lexical Backdoor Attacks on Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583348)
  - **Research question:** How does “Training-free Lexical Backdoor Attacks on Language Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive experiments on three dominant NLP tasks based on nine language models to demonstrate the effectiveness and universality of their attack.
- [Unnoticeable Backdoor Attacks on Graph Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583392)
  - **Research question:** How does “Unnoticeable Backdoor Attacks on Graph Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors select high-impact nodes for poisoning and use an adaptive trigger generator to mount effective graph backdoors under a small attack budget.
- [Curriculum Graph Poisoning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583211)
  - **Research question:** How does “Curriculum Graph Poisoning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors revisit the bi-level optimization problem in graph poisoning and propose a novel graph poisoning method, termed Curriculum Graph Poisoning (CuGPo), inspired by curriculum learning.
- [MaSS: Model-agnostic, Semantic and Stealthy Data Poisoning Attack on Knowledge Graph Embedding](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583203)
  - **Research question:** How does “MaSS: Model-agnostic, Semantic and Stealthy Data Poisoning Attack on Knowledge Graph Embedding” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Based on this, the authors develop a novel and efficient algorithm to search paths that maximize the attack goal, satisfy certain semantic constraints, and preserve certain stealthiness, i.e., the normal functionality of the target KGE will not be influenced although it predicts wrong facts given certain queries.

## 2. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Membership Inference Attacks Against Sequential Recommender Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583447)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Membership Inference Attacks Against Sequential Recommender Systems” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these difficulties, the authors propose a Membership Inference Attack framework against sequential recommenders based on Model Extraction(ME-MIA).
- [NetGuard: Protecting Commercial Web APIs from Model Inversion Attacks using GAN-generated Fake Samples](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583224)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “NetGuard: Protecting Commercial Web APIs from Model Inversion Attacks using GAN-generated Fake Samples” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors proposed NetGuard, a novel utility-aware defense methodology against model inversion attacks (MIAs).
- [AgrEvader: Poisoning Membership Inference against Byzantine-robust Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583542)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “AgrEvader: Poisoning Membership Inference against Byzantine-robust Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct a benchmark evaluation to assess the performance of PMIA against the Byzantine-robust FL setting that is specifically designed to mitigate poisoning attacks.
- [Sanitizing Sentence Embeddings (and Labels) for Local Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583512)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Sanitizing Sentence Embeddings (and Labels) for Local Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show how to achieve metric-based local DP (LDP) by sanitizing (high-dimensional) sentence embedding, extracted by LMs and much smaller than gradients.
- [Ginver: Generative Model Inversion Attacks Against Collaborative Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583306)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Ginver: Generative Model Inversion Attacks Against Collaborative Inference” investigate?
  - **What they did:** However, the authors show in this paper that the adversary can restore the victim’s model input by exploiting the output of the victim’s local model.
- [Heterogeneous Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding Learning and Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583305)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Heterogeneous Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding Learning and Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose FedLU, a novel FL framework for heterogeneous KG embedding learning and unlearning.
- [Unlearning Graph Classifiers with Limited Data Resources](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583547)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Unlearning Graph Classifiers with Limited Data Resources” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this issue, the authors initiate the study of unlearning the Graph Scattering Transform (GST), a mathematical framework that is efficient, provably stable under feature or graph topology perturbations, and offers graph classification performance comparable to that of GNNs.
- [Quantifying and Defending against Privacy Threats on Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583450)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Quantifying and Defending against Privacy Threats on Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct the first holistic study of the privacy threat on FKGE from both attack and defense perspectives.
- [Interaction-level Membership Inference Attack Against Federated Recommender Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583359)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Interaction-level Membership Inference Attack Against Federated Recommender Systems” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors further investigate the privacy problem of user behavior data (i.e., user-item interactions) in FedRecs.
- [Provenance of Training without Training Data: Towards Privacy-Preserving DNN Model Ownership Verification](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583198)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Provenance of Training without Training Data: Towards Privacy-Preserving DNN Model Ownership Verification” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel Provenance of Training (PoT) scheme, the first empirical study towards verifying DNN model ownership without accessing any original dataset while being robust against existing attacks.
- [GIF: A General Graph Unlearning Strategy via Influence Function](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583521)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “GIF: A General Graph Unlearning Strategy via Influence Function” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors first present a unified problem formulation of diverse graph unlearning tasks w.r.t. node, edge, and feature.

## 3. Secure and Verifiable AI Systems

- [Meteor: Improved Secure 3-Party Neural Network Inference with Reducing Online Communication Costs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583272)
  - **Research question:** How does “Meteor: Improved Secure 3-Party Neural Network Inference with Reducing Online Communication Costs” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Meteor, an online communication-efficient and fast secure 3-party computation neural network inference system aginst semi-honest adversary in honest-majority.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [IRWArt: Levering Watermarking Performance for Protecting High-quality Artwork Images](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583489)
  - **Research question:** How does “IRWArt: Levering Watermarking Performance for Protecting High-quality Artwork Images” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To provide high-level protection for valuable artwork images, the authors propose a novel invisible robust watermarking framework, dubbed as IRWArt.
- [Transferring Audio Deepfake Detection Capability across Languages](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583222)
  - **Research question:** How does “Transferring Audio Deepfake Detection Capability across Languages” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Based on the observation, the authors first construct a first-of-its-kind cross-lingual evaluation dataset including heterogeneous spoofed speech uttered in the two most widely spoken languages, then explored domain adaptation (DA) techniques to transfer the artifacts detection capability and propose effective and practical DA strategies fitting the cross-lingual scenario.

## 5. Other AI Security and Privacy

- [Minimum Topology Attacks for Graph Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583509)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Minimum Topology Attacks for Graph Neural Networks” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose an attack model, named MiBTack, based on a dynamic projected gradient descent algorithm, which can effectively solve the involving non-convex constraint optimization on discrete topology.
- [Anti-FakeU: Defending Shilling Attacks on Graph Neural Network based Recommender Model](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583289)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Anti-FakeU: Defending Shilling Attacks on Graph Neural Network based Recommender Model” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors construct a user-user graph to capture the patterns of malicious behaviors and design a novel GNN based detector to identify fake users.
