# INFOCOM 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [INFOCOM 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://infocom2023.ieee-infocom.org/program/accepted-paper-list-main-conference) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Mind Your Heart: Stealthy Backdoor Attack on Dynamic Deep Neural Network in Edge Computing](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10229092)
  - **Research question:** How does “Mind Your Heart: Stealthy Backdoor Attack on Dynamic Deep Neural Network in Edge Computing” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive experiments to prove the effectiveness of their attack on three structures (ResNet-56, VGG-16, and MobileNet) with four datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN, GTSRB, and Tiny-ImageNet) and their backdoor is stealthy to evade multiple state-of-the-art backdoor detection or removal methods.
- [OBLIVION: Poisoning Federated Learning by Inducing Catastrophic Forgetting](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228981)
  - **Research question:** How does “OBLIVION: Poisoning Federated Learning by Inducing Catastrophic Forgetting” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop an advanced model poisoning attack against defensive aggregation rules.

## 2. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Asynchronous Federated Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10229075)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Asynchronous Federated Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present the design and implementation of Knot, a new clustered aggregation mechanism custom-tailored to asynchronous federated learning.
- [Mixup Training for Generative Models to Defend Membership Inference Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10229036)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Mixup Training for Generative Models to Defend Membership Inference Attacks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a mixup training method for generative adversarial networks (GANs) as a defense against MIAs.
- [More than Enough is Too Much: Adaptive Defenses against Gradient Leakage in Production Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228919)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “More than Enough is Too Much: Adaptive Defenses against Gradient Leakage in Production Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present key priors required to make the attack possible or stronger, such as a narrow distribution of initial model weights, as well as inversion at early stages of training.
- [Privacy as a Resource in Differentially Private Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228953)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Privacy as a Resource in Differentially Private Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel DP-enhanced FL platform that treats privacy as a resource and schedules multiple FL jobs across sensitive data.
- [Privacy-preserving Stable Crowdsensing Data Trading for Unknown Market](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228966)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Privacy-preserving Stable Crowdsensing Data Trading for Unknown Market” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors model the market as a differentially private multi-player bandit problem and propose DPS-CB, which combines differential privacy, stable matching, and a competing-bandit strategy.

## 3. Secure and Verifiable AI Systems

- [FedSDG-FS: Efficient and Secure Feature Selection for Vertical Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228895)
  - **Research question:** How does “FedSDG-FS: Efficient and Secure Feature Selection for Vertical Federated Learning” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** To bridge this gap, the authors propose the Federated Stochastic Dual-Gate based Feature Selection (FedSDG-FS) approach.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [Nowhere to Hide: Detecting Live Video Forgery via Vision-WiFi Silhouette Correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228947)
  - **Research question:** How does “Nowhere to Hide: Detecting Live Video Forgery via Vision-WiFi Silhouette Correspondence” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To secure existing surveillance systems, the authors propose a vision-WiFi cross-modal video forgery detection system named WiSil.

## 5. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [Spotting Deep Neural Network Vulnerabilities in Mobile Traffic Forecasting with an Explainable AI Lens](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom53939.2023.10228989)
  - **Research question:** How does “Spotting Deep Neural Network Vulnerabilities in Mobile Traffic Forecasting with an Explainable AI Lens” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct a first in-depth study of the vulnerabilities of DNNs for large-scale mobile traffic forecasting.
