# INFOCOM 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [INFOCOM 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://infocom2024.ieee-infocom.org/) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Explanation-Guided Backdoor Attacks on Model-Agnostic RF Fingerprinting](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621289)
  - **Research question:** How does “Explanation-Guided Backdoor Attacks on Model-Agnostic RF Fingerprinting” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors analyze the susceptibility of DNN-based RF fingerprinting to backdoor attacks, focusing on a more practical scenario where attackers lack access to control model gradients and training processes.

## 2. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Breaking Secure Aggregation: Label Leakage from Aggregated Gradients in Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621090)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Breaking Secure Aggregation: Label Leakage from Aggregated Gradients in Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a stealthy label inference attack to bypass SA and recover individual clients’ private labels.
- [Strategic Data Revocation in Federated Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621201)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Strategic Data Revocation in Federated Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Additionally, the authors demonstrate that positive externalities may exist between users’ data revocation decisions when users incur unlearning costs, while this is not the case when their unlearning costs are negligible.
- [Privacy-Preserving Data Evaluation via Functional Encryption, Revisited](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621262)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Privacy-Preserving Data Evaluation via Functional Encryption, Revisited” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To combat this, the authors introduce a novel cryptographic primitive known as labeled function-hiding inner-product encrypted.
- [DPBalance: Efficient and Fair Privacy Budget Scheduling for Federated Learning as a Service](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621227)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “DPBalance: Efficient and Fair Privacy Budget Scheduling for Federated Learning as a Service” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose DPBalance, a novel privacy budget scheduling mechanism that jointly optimizes both efficiency and fairness.
- [X-Stream: A Flexible, Adaptive Video Transformer for Privacy-Preserving Video Stream Analytics](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621341)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “X-Stream: A Flexible, Adaptive Video Transformer for Privacy-Preserving Video Stream Analytics” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present X-Stream, a privacy-preserving video transformer that achieves flexibility and efficiency for a large variety of VSA tasks.

## 3. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [Efficient and Effective In-Vehicle Intrusion Detection System using Binarized Convolutional Neural Network](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621400)
  - **Research question:** How does “Efficient and Effective In-Vehicle Intrusion Detection System using Binarized Convolutional Neural Network” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors introduce a novel Binarized Convolutional Neural Network (BCNN)-based IDS, designed to exploit the temporal and spatial characteristics of CAN messages to achieve both efficiency and detection accuracy.

## 4. Other AI Security and Privacy

- [Deep Learning Models as Moving Targets to Counter Modulation Classification Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/infocom52122.2024.10621413)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Deep Learning Models as Moving Targets to Counter Modulation Classification Attacks” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a moving-target defense framework to support a novel modulation-masking mechanism the authors develop against advanced and persistent MC attacks.
