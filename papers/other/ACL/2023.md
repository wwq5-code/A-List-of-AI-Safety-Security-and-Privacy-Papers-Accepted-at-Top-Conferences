# ACL 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [ACL 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://aclanthology.org/events/acl-2023/) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [DIP: Dead code Insertion based Black-box Attack for Programming Language Model](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.430/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “DIP: Dead code Insertion based Black-box Attack for Programming Language Model” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose DIP (Dead code Insertion based Black-box Attack for Programming Language Model), a high-performance and effective black-box attack method to generate adversarial examples using dead code insertion.
- [Adversarial Robustness of Prompt-based Few-Shot Learning for Natural Language Understanding](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.138/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Adversarial Robustness of Prompt-based Few-Shot Learning for Natural Language Understanding” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct an extensive study of several state-of-the-art FSL methods to assess their robustness to adversarial perturbations.
- [Modeling Adversarial Attack on Pre-trained Language Models as Sequential Decision Making](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.461/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Modeling Adversarial Attack on Pre-trained Language Models as Sequential Decision Making” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Considering the attack process can only receive the final state without any direct intermediate signals, the authors propose to use reinforcement learning to find an appropriate sequential attack path to generate adversaries, named SDM-ATTACK.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [White-Box Multi-Objective Adversarial Attack on Dialogue Generation](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.100/)
  - **Research question:** How does “White-Box Multi-Objective Adversarial Attack on Dialogue Generation” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose a white-box multi-objective attack method called DGSlow.
- [A Gradient Control Method for Backdoor Attacks on Parameter-Efficient Tuning](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.194/)
  - **Research question:** How does “A Gradient Control Method for Backdoor Attacks on Parameter-Efficient Tuning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Based on this finding, the authors propose a gradient control method to consolidate the attack effect, comprising two strategies.
- [Multi-target Backdoor Attacks for Code Pre-trained Models](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.399/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Multi-target Backdoor Attacks for Code Pre-trained Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluate their approach on two code understanding tasks and three code generation tasks over seven datasets.
- [BITE: Textual Backdoor Attacks with Iterative Trigger Injection](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.725/)
  - **Research question:** How does “BITE: Textual Backdoor Attacks with Iterative Trigger Injection” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate that it is possible to design a backdoor attack that is both stealthy (i.e., hard to notice) and effective (i.e., has a high attack success rate).
- [NOTABLE: Transferable Backdoor Attacks Against Prompt-based NLP Models](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.867/)
  - **Research question:** How does “NOTABLE: Transferable Backdoor Attacks Against Prompt-based NLP Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose transferable backdoor attacks against prompt-based models, called NOTABLE, which is independent of downstream tasks and prompting strategies.
- [In and Out-of-Domain Text Adversarial Robustness via Label Smoothing](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.58/)
  - **Research question:** How does “In and Out-of-Domain Text Adversarial Robustness via Label Smoothing” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the adversarial robustness provided by label smoothing strategies in foundational models for diverse NLP tasks in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings.
- [Layerwise universal adversarial attack on NLP models](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.10/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Layerwise universal adversarial attack on NLP models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** On the example of three transformer models and three datasets from the GLUE benchmark, the authors demonstrate that their method provides better transferability in a model-to-model setting with an average gain of 9.3% in the fooling rate over the baseline.
- [Diffusion Theory as a Scalpel: Detecting and Purifying Poisonous Dimensions in Pre-trained Language Models Caused by Backdoor or Bias](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.157/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Diffusion Theory as a Scalpel: Detecting and Purifying Poisonous Dimensions in Pre-trained Language Models Caused by Backdoor or Bias” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To settle this issue, the authors propose the Fine-purifying approach, which utilizes the diffusion theory to study the dynamic process of fine-tuning for finding potentially poisonous dimensions.
- [Maximum Entropy Loss, the Silver Bullet Targeting Backdoor Attacks in Pre-trained Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.237/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Maximum Entropy Loss, the Silver Bullet Targeting Backdoor Attacks in Pre-trained Language Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** In extension, the authors explore the relationship between intended backdoor attacks and unintended dataset bias, and demonstrate the feasibility of the maximum entropy principle in de-biasing.
- [Bridge the Gap Between CV and NLP! A Gradient-based Textual Adversarial Attack Framework](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.446/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Bridge the Gap Between CV and NLP! A Gradient-based Textual Adversarial Attack Framework” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Therefore, the authors perform the more challenging transfer black-box attack and conduct comprehensive experiments to evaluate their attack algorithm with several models on three benchmark datasets.
- [Defending against Insertion-based Textual Backdoor Attacks via Attribution](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.561/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Defending against Insertion-based Textual Backdoor Attacks via Attribution” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose AttDef, an efficient attribution-based pipeline to defend against two insertion-based poisoning attacks, BadNL and InSent.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Synthetic Text Generation with Differential Privacy: A Simple and Practical Recipe](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.74/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Synthetic Text Generation with Differential Privacy: A Simple and Practical Recipe” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Through extensive empirical analyses on both benchmark and private customer data, the authors demonstrate that their method produces synthetic text that is competitive in terms of utility with its non-private counterpart, meanwhile providing strong protection against potential privacy leakages.
- [Privacy-Preserving Domain Adaptation of Semantic Parsers](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.271/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Privacy-Preserving Domain Adaptation of Semantic Parsers” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose a two-stage Differentially Private (DP) generation method which first generates latent semantic parses, and then generates utterances based on the parses.
- [ETHICIST: Targeted Training Data Extraction Through Loss Smoothed Soft Prompting and Calibrated Confidence Estimation](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.709/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “ETHICIST: Targeted Training Data Extraction Through Loss Smoothed Soft Prompting and Calibrated Confidence Estimation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a method named Ethicist for targeted training data extraction through loss smoothed soft prompting and calibrated confidence estimation, investigating how to recover the suffix in the training data when given a prefix.
- [KGA: A General Machine Unlearning Framework Based on Knowledge Gap Alignment](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.740/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “KGA: A General Machine Unlearning Framework Based on Knowledge Gap Alignment” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a general unlearning framework called KGA to induce forgetfulness.
- [Knowledge Unlearning for Mitigating Privacy Risks in Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.805/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Knowledge Unlearning for Mitigating Privacy Risks in Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** By showing comparisons with previous methods known to mitigate privacy risks for LMs, the authors show that their approach can give a stronger empirical privacy guarantee in scenarios where the data vulnerable to extraction attacks are known a priori while being much more efficient and robust.
- [PLUE: Language Understanding Evaluation Benchmark for Privacy Policies in English](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.31/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “PLUE: Language Understanding Evaluation Benchmark for Privacy Policies in English” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors introduce the Privacy Policy Language Understanding Evaluation (PLUE) benchmark, a multi-task benchmark for evaluating the privacy policy language understanding across various tasks.
- [TextObfuscator: Making Pre-trained Language Model a Privacy Protector via Obfuscating Word Representations](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.337/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “TextObfuscator: Making Pre-trained Language Model a Privacy Protector via Obfuscating Word Representations” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose TextObfuscator, a novel framework for protecting inference privacy by applying random perturbations to clustered representations.
- [A Customized Text Sanitization Mechanism with Differential Privacy](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.355/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Customized Text Sanitization Mechanism with Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these limitations, the authors propose a novel Customized Text sanitization (CusText) mechanism based on the original 𝜖 -differential privacy (DP) definition, which is compatible with any similarity measure.
- [Trade-Offs Between Fairness and Privacy in Language Modeling](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.434/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Trade-Offs Between Fairness and Privacy in Language Modeling” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct an extensive set of experiments that include bias detection, privacy attacks, language modeling, and performance on downstream tasks.
- [Membership Inference Attacks against Language Models via Neighbourhood Comparison](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.719/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Membership Inference Attacks against Language Models via Neighbourhood Comparison” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that, in addition to being competitive with reference-based attacks that have perfect knowledge about the training data distribution, their attack clearly outperforms existing reference-free attacks as well as reference-based attacks with imperfect knowledge, which demonstrates the need for a reevaluation of the threat model of adversarial attacks.
- [DP-BART for Privatized Text Rewriting under Local Differential Privacy](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.874/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “DP-BART for Privatized Text Rewriting under Local Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new system ‘DP-BART’ that largely outperforms existing LDP systems.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [Robust Multi-bit Natural Language Watermarking through Invariant Features](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.117/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Robust Multi-bit Natural Language Watermarking through Invariant Features” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Through a systematic analysis of the possible sources of errors, the authors further propose a corruption-resistant infill model.
- [Are You Copying My Model? Protecting the Copyright of Large Language Models for EaaS via Backdoor Watermark](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.423/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Are You Copying My Model? Protecting the Copyright of Large Language Models for EaaS via Backdoor Watermark” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To protect the copyright of LLMs for EaaS, the authors propose an Embedding Watermark method called {pasted macro ‘METHOD’} that implants backdoors on embeddings.

## 5. AI Safety, Alignment, and Responsible AI

- [SafeConv: Explaining and Correcting Conversational Unsafe Behavior](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.2/)
  - **Research question:** How does “SafeConv: Explaining and Correcting Conversational Unsafe Behavior” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors construct SafeConv with utterance-level safety labels, unsafe spans, and safer alternative responses, then train a framework to detect, explain, and correct unsafe conversational behavior.
- [Query-Efficient Black-Box Red Teaming via Bayesian Optimization](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.646/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Query-Efficient Black-Box Red Teaming via Bayesian Optimization” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose Bayesian red teaming (BRT), novel query-efficient black-box red teaming methods based on Bayesian optimization, which iteratively identify diverse positive test cases leading to model failures by utilizing the pre-defined user input pool and the past evaluations.
- [Unlearning Bias in Language Models by Partitioning Gradients](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.375/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Unlearning Bias in Language Models by Partitioning Gradients” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose a new technique called partitioned contrastive gradient unlearning (PCGU), a gray-box method for debiasing pretrained masked language models.
