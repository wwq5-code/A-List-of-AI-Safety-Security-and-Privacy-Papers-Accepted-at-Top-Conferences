# ACL 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [ACL 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://aclanthology.org/events/acl-2024/) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [GradSafe: Detecting Jailbreak Prompts for LLMs via Safety-Critical Gradient Analysis](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.30/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “GradSafe: Detecting Jailbreak Prompts for LLMs via Safety-Critical Gradient Analysis” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose GradSafe, which effectively detects jailbreak prompts by scrutinizing the gradients of safety-critical parameters in LLMs.
- [GSM-Plus: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Evaluating the Robustness of LLMs as Mathematical Problem Solvers](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.163/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “GSM-Plus: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Evaluating the Robustness of LLMs as Mathematical Problem Solvers” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the adversarial grade school math (GSM-Plus) dataset, an extension of GSM8K augmented with various mathematical perturbations.
- [Jailbreak Open-Sourced Large Language Models via Enforced Decoding](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.299/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Jailbreak Open-Sourced Large Language Models via Enforced Decoding” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluate their method on 4 open-sourced LLMs accessible publicly and their finding highlights the need for more advanced mitigation strategies for open-sourced LLMs.
- [SafeDecoding: Defending against Jailbreak Attacks via Safety-Aware Decoding](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.303/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “SafeDecoding: Defending against Jailbreak Attacks via Safety-Aware Decoding” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors analyze tokens, which are the smallest unit of text that can be processed by LLMs and make the following observations: (1) probabilities of tokens representing harmful responses are higher than those of harmless responses, and (2) responses containing safety disclaimers appear among the top tokens when token probabilities are sorted in descending order.
- [Stealthy Attack on Large Language Model based Recommendation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.318/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Stealthy Attack on Large Language Model based Recommendation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate that attackers can significantly boost an item’s exposure by merely altering its textual content during the testing phase, without requiring direct interference with the model’s training process.
- [Defending Large Language Models Against Jailbreaking Attacks Through Goal Prioritization](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.481/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Defending Large Language Models Against Jailbreaking Attacks Through Goal Prioritization” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Accordingly, the authors propose to integrate goal prioritization at both training and inference stages to counteract.
- [VulLibGen: Generating Names of Vulnerability-Affected Packages via a Large Language Model](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.527/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “VulLibGen: Generating Names of Vulnerability-Affected Packages via a Large Language Model” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this limitation, the authors propose VulLibGen, the first method to use LLM for affected package identification.
- [Defending Against Alignment-Breaking Attacks via Robustly Aligned LLM](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.568/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Defending Against Alignment-Breaking Attacks via Robustly Aligned LLM” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a Robustly Aligned LLM (RA-LLM) to defend against potential alignment-breaking attacks.
- [PRP: Propagating Universal Perturbations to Attack Large Language Model Guard-Rails](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.591/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “PRP: Propagating Universal Perturbations to Attack Large Language Model Guard-Rails” investigate?
  - **What they did:** More recent LLMs often incorporate an additional layer of defense, a Guard Model, which is a second LLM that is designed to check and moderate the output response of the primary LLM.
- [How Johnny Can Persuade LLMs to Jailbreak Them: Rethinking Persuasion to Challenge AI Safety by Humanizing LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.773/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “How Johnny Can Persuade LLMs to Jailbreak Them: Rethinking Persuasion to Challenge AI Safety by Humanizing LLMs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Specifically, the authors study how to persuade LLMs to jailbreak them.
- [ArtPrompt: ASCII Art-based Jailbreak Attacks against Aligned LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.809/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “ArtPrompt: ASCII Art-based Jailbreak Attacks against Aligned LLMs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel ASCII art-based jailbreak attack and introduce a comprehensive benchmark Vision-in-Text Challenge (ViTC) to evaluate the capabilities of LLMs in recognizing prompts that cannot be solely interpreted by semantics.
- [Emulated Disalignment: Safety Alignment for Large Language Models May Backfire!](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.842/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Emulated Disalignment: Safety Alignment for Large Language Models May Backfire” investigate?
  - **What they did:** However, this paper introduces a training-free attack method capable of reversing safety alignment, converting the outcomes of stronger alignment into greater potential for harm by accessing only LLM output token distributions.
- [OpenEval: Benchmarking Chinese LLMs across Capability, Alignment and Safety](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.19/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “OpenEval: Benchmarking Chinese LLMs across Capability, Alignment and Safety” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this gap, the authors introduce OpenEval, an evaluation testbed that benchmarks Chinese LLMs across capability, alignment and safety.
- [UNIWIZ: A Unified Large Language Model Orchestrated Wizard for Safe Knowledge Grounded Conversations](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.102/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “UNIWIZ: A Unified Large Language Model Orchestrated Wizard for Safe Knowledge Grounded Conversations” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these challenges, the authors introduce UniWiz, a novel 2-step data orchestration framework that unifies safety and knowledge data generation.
- [The Language Barrier: Dissecting Safety Challenges of LLMs in Multilingual Contexts](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.156/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “The Language Barrier: Dissecting Safety Challenges of LLMs in Multilingual Contexts” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To understand where the discrepancy can be attributed, the authors study the effect of instruction tuning with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or supervised finetuning (SFT) on the HH-RLHF dataset.
- [Red Teaming Visual Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.198/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Red Teaming Visual Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To explore this problem, the authors present a novel red teaming dataset RTVLM, which encompasses 12 subtasks (e.g., image misleading, multi-modal jailbreaking, face fairness, etc) under 4 primary aspects (faithfulness, privacy, safety, fairness).
- [Play Guessing Game with LLM: Indirect Jailbreak Attack with Implicit Clues](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.304/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Play Guessing Game with LLM: Indirect Jailbreak Attack with Implicit Clues” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose an indirect jailbreak attack approach, Puzzler, which can bypass the LLM’s defensive strategies and obtain malicious response by implicitly providing LLMs with some clues about the original malicious query.
- [All Languages Matter: On the Multilingual Safety of LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.349/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “All Languages Matter: On the Multilingual Safety of LLMs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors build the first multilingual safety benchmark for LLMs, XSafety, in response to the global deployment of LLMs in practice.
- [A Comprehensive Study of Jailbreak Attack versus Defense for Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.443/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “A Comprehensive Study of Jailbreak Attack versus Defense for Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors meticulously investigate nine attack techniques and seven defense techniques applied across three distinct language models: Vicuna, LLama, and GPT-3.5 Turbo.
- [On the Vulnerability of Safety Alignment in Open-Access LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.549/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “On the Vulnerability of Safety Alignment in Open-Access LLMs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Through reverse alignment, achieved by accessing model parameters, the authors show the feasibility of efficiently fine-tuning LLMs to undermine their inherent safeguards.
- [SpeechGuard: Exploring the Adversarial Robustness of Multi-modal Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.596/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “SpeechGuard: Exploring the Adversarial Robustness of Multi-modal Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate the potential vulnerabilities of such instruction-following speech-language models to adversarial attacks and jailbreaking.
- [Chaos with Keywords: Exposing Large Language Models Sycophancy to Misleading Keywords and Evaluating Defense Strategies](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.755/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Chaos with Keywords: Exposing Large Language Models Sycophancy to Misleading Keywords and Evaluating Defense Strategies” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors explore the sycophantic tendencies of Large Language Models (LLMs), where these models tend to provide answers that match what users want to hear, even if they are not entirely correct.
- [The Art of Defending: A Systematic Evaluation and Analysis of LLM Defense Strategies on Safety and Over-Defensiveness](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.776/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “The Art of Defending: A Systematic Evaluation and Analysis of LLM Defense Strategies on Safety and Over-Defensiveness” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors compile a large and diverse collection of safe and unsafe prompts, design precise evaluation methodology, and study the efficacy of various LLM defense strategies on multiple state-of-the-art LLMs.
- [Defending LLMs against Jailbreaking Attacks via Backtranslation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.948/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Defending LLMs against Jailbreaking Attacks via Backtranslation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new method for defending LLMs against jailbreaking attacks by “backtranslation”.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Extreme Miscalibration and the Illusion of Adversarial Robustness](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.137/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Extreme Miscalibration and the Illusion of Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that this observed gain in robustness is an illusion of robustness (IOR), and demonstrate how an adversary can perform various forms of test-time temperature calibration to nullify the aforementioned interference and allow the adversarial attack to find adversarial examples.
- [RLHFPoison: Reward Poisoning Attack for Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback in Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.140/)
  - **Research question:** How does “RLHFPoison: Reward Poisoning Attack for Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback in Large Language Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To assess the red-teaming of RLHF against human preference data poisoning, the authors propose RankPoison, a poisoning attack method on candidates’ selection of preference rank flipping to reach certain malicious behaviors (e.g., generating longer sequences, which can increase the computational cost).
- [Acquiring Clean Language Models from Backdoor Poisoned Datasets by Downscaling Frequency Space](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.441/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Acquiring Clean Language Models from Backdoor Poisoned Datasets by Downscaling Frequency Space” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate the learning mechanisms of backdoor LMs in the frequency space by Fourier analysis.
- [BadAgent: Inserting and Activating Backdoor Attacks in LLM Agents](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.530/)
  - **Research question:** How does “BadAgent: Inserting and Activating Backdoor Attacks in LLM Agents” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** However, the authors show that such methods are vulnerable to their proposed backdoor attacks named BadAgent on various agent tasks, where a backdoor can be embedded by fine-tuning on the backdoor data.
- [BadActs: A Universal Backdoor Defense in the Activation Space](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.317/)
  - **Research question:** How does “BadActs: A Universal Backdoor Defense in the Activation Space” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors introduce a universal backdoor defense that purifies backdoor samples in the activation space by drawing abnormal activations towards optimized minimum clean activation distribution intervals.
- [UOR: Universal Backdoor Attacks on Pre-trained Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.468/)
  - **Research question:** How does “UOR: Universal Backdoor Attacks on Pre-trained Language Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new backdoor attack method called UOR, which overcomes these limitations by turning manual selection into automatic optimization.
- [A Curious Case of Searching for the Correlation between Training Data and Adversarial Robustness of Transformer Textual Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.800/)
  - **Research question:** How does “A Curious Case of Searching for the Correlation between Training Data and Adversarial Robustness of Transformer Textual Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Second, their framework can be used as a fast and effective additional tool for robustness evaluation since it (a) saves 30x-193x runtime compared to the traditional technique, (b) is transferable across models, (c) can be used under adversarial training, and (d) robust to statistical randomness.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [PrivLM-Bench: A Multi-level Privacy Evaluation Benchmark for Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.4/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “PrivLM-Bench: A Multi-level Privacy Evaluation Benchmark for Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present PrivLM-Bench, a multi-perspective privacy evaluation benchmark to empirically and intuitively quantify the privacy leakage of LMs.
- [Exploring Memorization in Fine-tuned Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.216/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Exploring Memorization in Fine-tuned Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct the first comprehensive analysis to explore language models’ (LMs) memorization during fine-tuning across tasks.
- [Transferable Embedding Inversion Attack: Uncovering Privacy Risks in Text Embeddings without Model Queries](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.230/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Transferable Embedding Inversion Attack: Uncovering Privacy Risks in Text Embeddings without Model Queries” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate the privacy risks associated with text embeddings, focusing on the scenario where attackers cannot access the original embedding model.
- [Unlearning Traces the Influential Training Data of Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.343/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Unlearning Traces the Influential Training Data of Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** In the experiments, the authors examine if their methods can assess the influence of pretraining datasets on generating toxic, biased, and untruthful content.
- [Machine Unlearning of Pre-trained Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.457/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Machine Unlearning of Pre-trained Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Through rigorous evaluation using curated datasets from arXiv, books, and GitHub, the authors establish a robust benchmark for unlearning performance, demonstrating that these methods are over 10 5 times more computationally efficient than retraining.
- [Towards Privacy-Aware Sign Language Translation at Scale](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.467/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Towards Privacy-Aware Sign Language Translation at Scale” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a two-stage framework for privacy-aware SLT at scale that addresses both of these issues.
- [Reducing Privacy Risks in Online Self-Disclosures with Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.741/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Reducing Privacy Risks in Online Self-Disclosures with Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop a taxonomy of 19 self-disclosure categories and curate a large corpus consisting of 4.8K annotated disclosure spans.
- [Causal Estimation of Memorisation Profiles](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.834/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Causal Estimation of Memorisation Profiles” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The study fills an important gap in the literature, proposing a new, principled, and efficient method to estimate memorisation based on the difference-in-differences design from econometrics.
- [Learnable Privacy Neurons Localization in Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.25/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Learnable Privacy Neurons Localization in Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To bridge this gap, the authors introduce a pioneering method for pinpointing PII-sensitive neurons (privacy neurons) within LLMs.
- [Towards Safer Large Language Models through Machine Unlearning](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.107/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Towards Safer Large Language Models through Machine Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this gap, the authors introduce Selective Knowledge negation Unlearning (SKU), a novel unlearning framework for LLMs, designed to eliminate harmful knowledge while preserving utility on normal prompts.
- [The Good and The Bad: Exploring Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.267/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “The Good and The Bad: Exploring Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors conduct extensive empirical studies with novel attack methods, which demonstrate the vulnerability of RAG systems on leaking the private retrieval database.
- [Mitigating Privacy Seesaw in Large Language Models: Augmented Privacy Neuron Editing via Activation Patching](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.315/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Mitigating Privacy Seesaw in Large Language Models: Augmented Privacy Neuron Editing via Activation Patching” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Through comprehensive analysis, the authors identify the amount of targeted privacy data and the volume of edited privacy neurons as the two central triggers to this issue.
- [Deciphering the Impact of Pretraining Data on Large Language Models through Machine Unlearning](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.559/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Deciphering the Impact of Pretraining Data on Large Language Models through Machine Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors also identify a set of “high-impact data” such as Books that is significantly related to a set of model capabilities.
- [Generalization or Memorization: Data Contamination and Trustworthy Evaluation for Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.716/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Generalization or Memorization: Data Contamination and Trustworthy Evaluation for Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose CDD, which stands for Contamination Detection via output Distribution for LLMs.
- [Differentially Private Knowledge Distillation via Synthetic Text Generation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.769/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Differentially Private Knowledge Distillation via Synthetic Text Generation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose DistilDP: a novel differentially private knowledge distillation algorithm that exploits synthetic data generated by a differentially private teacher LLM.
- [SecFormer: Fast and Accurate Privacy-Preserving Inference for Transformer Models via SMPC](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.790)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SecFormer: Fast and Accurate Privacy-Preserving Inference for Transformer Models via SMPC” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this concern, the authors introduce a comprehensive PPI framework called SecFormer to achieve fast and accurate PPI for Transformer models.
- [Raccoon: Prompt Extraction Benchmark of LLM-Integrated Applications](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.791/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Raccoon: Prompt Extraction Benchmark of LLM-Integrated Applications” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To help mitigate this problem, the authors introduce the Raccoon benchmark which comprehensively evaluates a model’s susceptibility to prompt extraction attacks.
- [Generalisation First, Memorisation Second? Memorisation Localisation for Natural Language Classification Tasks](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.852/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Generalisation First, Memorisation Second? Memorisation Localisation for Natural Language Classification Tasks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Their results indicate that memorisation is a gradual process rather than a localised one, establish that memorisation is task-dependent, and give nuance to the generalisation first, memorisation second hypothesis.
- [Protecting Privacy Through Approximating Optimal Parameters for Sequence Unlearning in Language Models](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.14091)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Protecting Privacy Through Approximating Optimal Parameters for Sequence Unlearning in Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose Privacy Protection via Optimal Parameters (POP), a novel unlearning method that effectively forgets the target token sequences from the pretrained LM by applying optimal gradient updates to the parameters.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [MAGE: Machine-generated Text Detection in the Wild](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.3/)
  - **Research question:** How does “MAGE: Machine-generated Text Detection in the Wild” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors build a comprehensive testbed by gathering texts from diverse human writings and deepfake texts generated by different LLMs.
- [Stumbling Blocks: Stress Testing the Robustness of Machine-Generated Text Detectors Under Attacks](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.160/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Stumbling Blocks: Stress Testing the Robustness of Machine-Generated Text Detectors Under Attacks” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Further, the authors investigate the reasons behind these defects and propose initial out-of-the-box patches.
- [M4GT-Bench: Evaluation Benchmark for Black-Box Machine-Generated Text Detection](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.218/)
  - **Research question:** How does “M4GT-Bench: Evaluation Benchmark for Black-Box Machine-Generated Text Detection” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors address this problem by introducing a new benchmark based on a multilingual, multi-domain and multi-generator corpus of MGTs — M4GT-Bench.
- [Can Watermarks Survive Translation? On the Cross-lingual Consistency of Text Watermark for Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.226/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Can Watermarks Survive Translation? On the Cross-lingual Consistency of Text Watermark for Large Language Models” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the concept of cross-lingual consistency in text watermarking, which assesses the ability of text watermarks to maintain their effectiveness after being translated into other languages.
- [Who Wrote this Code? Watermarking for Code Generation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.268/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Who Wrote this Code? Watermarking for Code Generation” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Extending a logit-modifying watermark method, the authors propose Selective WatErmarking via Entropy Thresholding (SWEET), which enhances detection ability and mitigates code quality degeneration by removing low-entropy segments at generating and detecting watermarks.
- [Threads of Subtlety: Detecting Machine-Generated Texts Through Discourse Motifs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.298/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Threads of Subtlety: Detecting Machine-Generated Texts Through Discourse Motifs” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Introducing a novel methodology, the authors leverage hierarchical parse trees and recursive hypergraphs to unveil distinctive discourse patterns in texts produced by both LLMs and humans.
- [GumbelSoft: Diversified Language Model Watermarking via the GumbelMax-trick](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.315/)
  - **Research question:** How does “GumbelSoft: Diversified Language Model Watermarking via the GumbelMax-trick” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To overcome this limitation, the authors introduce a new type of GM watermark, the Logits-Addition watermark, as well as three variants that aim to enhance diversity, particularly the GumbelSoft watermark (i.e., the softmax variant of the Logits-Addition watermark).
- [Leveraging Machine-Generated Rationales to Facilitate Social Meaning Detection in Conversations](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.373/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Leveraging Machine-Generated Rationales to Facilitate Social Meaning Detection in Conversations” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a generalizable classification approach that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to facilitate the detection of implicitly encoded social meaning in conversations.
- [WatME: Towards Lossless Watermarking Through Lexical Redundancy](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.496/)
  - **Research question:** How does “WatME: Towards Lossless Watermarking Through Lexical Redundancy” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To address these challenges, the authors introduce Watermarking with Mutual Exclusion (WatME), a novel approach leveraging linguistic prior knowledge of inherent lexical redundancy in LLM vocabularies to seamlessly integrate watermarks.
- [An Entropy-based Text Watermarking Detection Method](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.630/)
  - **Research question:** How does “An Entropy-based Text Watermarking Detection Method” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** From the experiments, the authors demonstrate that their EWD can achieve better detection performance in low-entropy scenarios, and their method is also general and can be applied to texts with different entropy distributions.
- [RAID: A Shared Benchmark for Robust Evaluation of Machine-Generated Text Detectors](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.674/)
  - **Research question:** How does “RAID: A Shared Benchmark for Robust Evaluation of Machine-Generated Text Detectors” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors present RAID: the largest and most challenging benchmark dataset for machine-generated text detection.
- [WARDEN: Multi-Directional Backdoor Watermarks for Embedding-as-a-Service Copyright Protection](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.725/)
  - **Research question:** How does “WARDEN: Multi-Directional Backdoor Watermarks for Embedding-as-a-Service Copyright Protection” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Through the analysis of the recent watermarking strategy for EaaS, EmbMarker, the authors design a novel CSE (Clustering, Selection, Elimination) attack that removes the backdoor watermark while maintaining the high utility of embeddings, indicating that the previous watermarking approach can be breached.
