# ICLR 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [ICLR 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/papers.html) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [DensePure: Understanding Diffusion Models for Adversarial Robustness](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11007)
  - **Research question:** How does “DensePure: Understanding Diffusion Models for Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** By using the highest density point in the conditional distribution as the reversed sample, the authors identify the robust region of a given instance under the diffusion model's reverse process.
- [On Explaining Neural Network Robustness with Activation Path](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11195)
  - **Research question:** How does “On Explaining Neural Network Robustness with Activation Path” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that the boost of robust accuracy from randomized smoothing is the result of correcting the latter.
- [Squeeze Training for Adversarial Robustness](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11296)
  - **Research question:** How does “Squeeze Training for Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors highlight that some collaborative examples, nearly perceptually indistinguishable from both adversarial and benign examples yet show extremely lower prediction loss, can be utilized to enhance adversarial training.
- [Revisiting the Assumption of Latent Separability for Backdoor Defenses](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11430)
  - **Research question:** How does “Revisiting the Assumption of Latent Separability for Backdoor Defenses” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors design adaptive backdoor poisoning attacks to present counter-examples against this assumption.
- [Part-Based Models Improve Adversarial Robustness](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11504)
  - **Research question:** How does “Part-Based Models Improve Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that combining human prior knowledge with end-to-end learning can improve the robustness of deep neural networks by introducing a part-based model for object classification.
- [The Dark Side of AutoML: Towards Architectural Backdoor Search](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11495)
  - **Research question:** How does “The Dark Side of AutoML: Towards Architectural Backdoor Search” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Specifically, the authors present EVAS, a new attack that leverages NAS to find neural architectures with inherent backdoors and exploits such vulnerability using input-aware triggers.
- [Distilling Cognitive Backdoor Patterns within an Image](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/12155)
  - **Research question:** How does “Distilling Cognitive Backdoor Patterns within an Image” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive experiments to show that CD can robustly detect a wide range of advanced backdoor attacks.
- [UNICORN: A Unified Backdoor Trigger Inversion Framework](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11162)
  - **Research question:** How does “UNICORN: A Unified Backdoor Trigger Inversion Framework” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Then, it proposes a unified framework to invert backdoor triggers based on the formalization of triggers and the identified inner behaviors of backdoor models from their analysis.
- [Few-shot Backdoor Attacks via Neural Tangent Kernels](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11068)
  - **Research question:** How does “Few-shot Backdoor Attacks via Neural Tangent Kernels” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors further demonstrate a vulnerability in overparametrized deep neural networks, which is revealed by the shape of the neural tangent kernel.
- [Aligning Model and Macaque Inferior Temporal Cortex Representations Improves Model-to-Human Behavioral Alignment and Adversarial Robustness](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11087)
  - **Research question:** How does “Aligning Model and Macaque Inferior Temporal Cortex Representations Improves Model-to-Human Behavioral Alignment and Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors conducted chronic, large-scale multi-electrode recordings across the IT cortex in six non-human primates (rhesus macaques).
- [Revisiting Graph Adversarial Attack and Defense From a Data Distribution Perspective](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11886)
  - **Research question:** How does “Revisiting Graph Adversarial Attack and Defense From a Data Distribution Perspective” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Moreover, the authors design a fast attack method and a self-training defense method, which outperform the state-of-the-art methods and can effectively scale to large graphs like ogbn-arxiv.
- [Revisiting Robustness in Graph Machine Learning](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11062)
  - **Research question:** How does “Revisiting Robustness in Graph Machine Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this problem, the authors introduce a more principled notion of an adversarial graph, which is aware of semantic content change.
- [Is Adversarial Training Really a Silver Bullet for Mitigating Data Poisoning?](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11449)
  - **Research question:** How does “Is Adversarial Training Really a Silver Bullet for Mitigating Data Poisoning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate that for attacking a CIFAR-10 AT model under a reasonable setting with $\epsilon_\mathrm{adv}=\epsilon_\mathrm{poi}=8/255$, their EntF yields an accuracy drop of $13.31\%$, which is $7\times$ better than existing methods and equal to discarding $83\%$ training data.
- [(Certified!!) Adversarial Robustness for Free!](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11081)
  - **Research question:** How does “(Certified!!) Adversarial Robustness for Free” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show how to achieve state-of-the-art certified adversarial robustness to 2-norm bounded perturbations by relying exclusively on off-the-shelf pretrained models.
- [Clean-image Backdoor: Attacking Multi-label Models with Poisoned Labels Only](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11656)
  - **Research question:** How does “Clean-image Backdoor: Attacking Multi-label Models with Poisoned Labels Only” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors design a novel trigger exploration method to find convert and effective triggers to enhance the attack performance.
- [SCALE-UP: An Efficient Black-box Input-level Backdoor Detection via Analyzing Scaled Prediction Consistency](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11731)
  - **Research question:** How does “SCALE-UP: An Efficient Black-box Input-level Backdoor Detection via Analyzing Scaled Prediction Consistency” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a simple yet effective black-box input-level backdoor detection, called SCALE-UP, which requires only the predicted labels to alleviate this problem.
- [Exploring and Exploiting Decision Boundary Dynamics for Adversarial Robustness](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11455)
  - **Research question:** How does “Exploring and Exploiting Decision Boundary Dynamics for Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To understand this, the authors propose a continuous-time framework for quantifying the relative speed of the decision boundary with respect to each individual point.
- [Understanding Zero-shot Adversarial Robustness for Large-Scale Models](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11946)
  - **Research question:** How does “Understanding Zero-shot Adversarial Robustness for Large-Scale Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors identify and explore the problem of adapting large-scale models for zero-shot adversarial robustness.
- [FLIP: A Provable Defense Framework for Backdoor Mitigation in Federated Learning](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10762)
  - **Research question:** How does “FLIP: A Provable Defense Framework for Backdoor Mitigation in Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Moreover, the authors propose a trigger reverse engineering based defense and show that their method can achieve robustness improvement with guarantee (i.e., reducing the attack success rate) without affecting benign accuracy.
- [TrojText: Test-time Invisible Textual Trojan Insertion](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10756)
  - **Research question:** How does “TrojText: Test-time Invisible Textual Trojan Insertion” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a solution called TrojText, which aims to determine whether invisible textual Trojan attacks can be performed more efficiently and cost-effectively without training data.
- [Incompatibility Clustering as a Defense Against Backdoor Poisoning Attacks](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11486)
  - **Research question:** How does “Incompatibility Clustering as a Defense Against Backdoor Poisoning Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel clustering mechanism based on an incompatibility property between subsets of data that emerges during model training.
- [Indiscriminate Poisoning Attacks on Unsupervised Contrastive Learning](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11277)
  - **Research question:** How does “Indiscriminate Poisoning Attacks on Unsupervised Contrastive Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose Contrastive Poisoning (CP), the first effective such attack on CL.

## 2. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Effective passive membership inference attacks in federated learning against overparameterized models](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11045)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Effective passive membership inference attacks in federated learning against overparameterized models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Using this insight, the authors devise two attacks that are often little impacted by existing and proposed defenses.
- [CANIFE: Crafting Canaries for Empirical Privacy Measurement in Federated Learning](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11157)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “CANIFE: Crafting Canaries for Empirical Privacy Measurement in Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel method, CANIFE, that uses canaries - carefully crafted samples by a strong adversary to evaluate the empirical privacy of a training round.
- [A Statistical Framework for Personalized Federated Learning and Estimation: Theory, Algorithms, and Privacy](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10968)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Statistical Framework for Personalized Federated Learning and Estimation: Theory, Algorithms, and Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop novel private personalized estimation under this framework.
- [Efficient Model Updates for Approximate Unlearning of Graph-Structured Data](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11519)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Efficient Model Updates for Approximate Unlearning of Graph-Structured Data” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the first known approach for approximate graph unlearning with provable theoretical guarantees.
- [Disparate Impact in Differential Privacy from Gradient Misalignment](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/12111)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Disparate Impact in Differential Privacy from Gradient Misalignment” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the fine-grained causes of unfairness in DPSGD and identify gradient misalignment due to inequitable gradient clipping as the most significant source.
- [Quantifying Memorization Across Neural Language Models](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11469)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Quantifying Memorization Across Neural Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Memorization significantly grows as the authors increase (1) the capacity of a model, (2) the number of times an example has been duplicated, and (3) the number of tokens of context used to prompt the model.
- [Private Federated Learning Without a Trusted Server: Optimal Algorithms for Convex Losses](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11920)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Private Federated Learning Without a Trusted Server: Optimal Algorithms for Convex Losses” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Remarkably, the authors show that similar bounds are attainable for smooth losses with arbitrary heterogeneous silo data distributions, via an accelerated ISRL-DP algorithm.
- [Distributed Differential Privacy in Multi-Armed Bandits](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11384)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Distributed Differential Privacy in Multi-Armed Bandits” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors also show that their algorithm, when instantiated with Skellam noise and the secure protocol, ensures \emph{R\'{e}nyi differential privacy} -- a stronger notion than approximate DP -- under distributed trust model with a privacy cost of $O\!\left(\!\frac{K\sqrt{\log T}}{\epsilon}\!\right)\!$.
- [Exploring the Limits of Differentially Private Deep Learning with Group-wise Clipping](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11123)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Exploring the Limits of Differentially Private Deep Learning with Group-wise Clipping” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To reduce the compute time overhead of private learning, the authors show that per-layer clipping, where the gradient of each neural network layer is clipped separately, allows clipping to be performed in conjunction with backpropagation in differentially private optimization.
- [Individual Privacy Accounting with Gaussian Differential Privacy](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10727)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Individual Privacy Accounting with Gaussian Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors also show how to speed up the FFT-based individual DP accounting using the Plancherel theorem.
- [Learning to Linearize Deep Neural Networks for Secure and Efficient Private Inference](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/12068)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Learning to Linearize Deep Neural Networks for Secure and Efficient Private Inference” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors first present a novel measure of non-linearity layers’ ReLU sensitivity, enabling mitigation of the time-consuming manual efforts in identifying the same.
- [Canary in a Coalmine: Better Membership Inference with Ensembled Adversarial Queries](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11094)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Canary in a Coalmine: Better Membership Inference with Ensembled Adversarial Queries” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Membership inference algorithms approach this problem by using statistical techniques to discern whether a target sample was included in a model's training set.
- [Machine Unlearning of Federated Clusters](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11362)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Machine Unlearning of Federated Clusters” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce, for the first time, the problem of machine unlearning for FC, and propose an efficient unlearning mechanism for a customized secure FC framework.
- [Panning for Gold in Federated Learning: Targeted Text Extraction under Arbitrarily Large-Scale Aggregation](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10906)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Panning for Gold in Federated Learning: Targeted Text Extraction under Arbitrarily Large-Scale Aggregation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose the first attack on FL that achieves targeted extraction of sequences that contain privacy-critical phrases, whereby the authors employ maliciously modified parameters to allow the transformer itself to filter relevant sequences from aggregated user data and encode them in the gradient update.
- [GNNDelete: A General Strategy for Unlearning in Graph Neural Networks](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11746)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “GNNDelete: A General Strategy for Unlearning in Graph Neural Networks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these limitations, the authors introduce GNNDelete, a novel model-agnostic layer-wise operator that optimizes two critical properties, namely, Deleted Edge Consistency and Neighborhood Influence, for graph unlearning.
- [Share Your Representation Only: Guaranteed Improvement of the Privacy-Utility Tradeoff in Federated Learning](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10697)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Share Your Representation Only: Guaranteed Improvement of the Privacy-Utility Tradeoff in Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors empirically evaluate their method with the image classification task on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and EMNIST, and observe a significant performance improvement over the prior work under the same small privacy budget.
- [Regression with Label Differential Privacy](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10740)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Regression with Label Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the task of training regression models with the guarantee of label differential privacy (DP).
- [Decepticons: Corrupted Transformers Breach Privacy in Federated Learning for Language Models](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/10839)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Decepticons: Corrupted Transformers Breach Privacy in Federated Learning for Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel attack that reveals private user text by deploying malicious parameter vectors, and which succeeds even with mini-batches, multiple users, and long sequences.
