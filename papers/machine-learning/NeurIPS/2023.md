# NeurIPS 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [NeurIPS 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/papers.html) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [On Evaluating Adversarial Robustness of Large Vision-Language Models](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/69997)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “On Evaluating Adversarial Robustness of Large Vision-Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose evaluating the robustness of open-source large VLMs in the most realistic and high-risk setting, where adversaries have only black-box system access and seek to deceive the model into returning the targeted responses.
- [Jailbroken: How Does LLM Safety Training Fail?](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70702)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Jailbroken: How Does LLM Safety Training Fail” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Going beyond recognition of the issue, the authors investigate why such attacks succeed and how they can be created.
- [BeaverTails: Towards Improved Safety Alignment of LLM via a Human-Preference Dataset](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/73512)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “BeaverTails: Towards Improved Safety Alignment of LLM via a Human-Preference Dataset” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the BeaverTails dataset, aimed at fostering research on safety alignment in large language models (LLMs).

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Label Poisoning is All You Need](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70392)
  - **Research question:** How does “Label Poisoning is All You Need” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a novel approach to design label-only backdoor attacks, which the authors call FLIP, and demonstrate its strengths on three datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet) and four architectures (ResNet-32, ResNet-18, VGG-19, and Vision Transformer).
- [Spatial-frequency channels, shape bias, and adversarial robustness](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71982)
  - **Research question:** How does “Spatial-frequency channels, shape bias, and adversarial robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Here, the authors introduce critical band masking as a task for network-human comparison and test 14 humans and 76 neural networks on 16-way ImageNet categorization in the presence of narrowband noise.
- [On Transfer of Adversarial Robustness from Pretraining to Downstream Tasks](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72393)
  - **Research question:** How does “On Transfer of Adversarial Robustness from Pretraining to Downstream Tasks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate that the robustness of a linear predictor on downstream tasks can be constrained by the robustness of its underlying representation, regardless of the protocol used for pretraining.
- [IBA: Towards Irreversible Backdoor Attacks in Federated Learning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71079)
  - **Research question:** How does “IBA: Towards Irreversible Backdoor Attacks in Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Finally, the authors evaluate the proposed attack framework on several benchmark datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny ImageNet, and achieved high success rates while simultaneously bypassing existing backdoor defenses and achieving a more durable backdoor effect compared to other backdoor attacks.
- [Adversarial Examples Might be Avoidable: The Role of Data Concentration in Adversarial Robustness](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72069)
  - **Research question:** How does “Adversarial Examples Might be Avoidable: The Role of Data Concentration in Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors further demonstrate that, for a data distribution concentrated on a union of low-dimensional linear subspaces, utilizing structure in data naturally leads to classifiers that enjoy data-dependent polyhedral robustness guarantees, improving upon methods for provable certification in certain regimes.
- [Provable Adversarial Robustness for Group Equivariant Tasks: Graphs, Point Clouds, Molecules, and More](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70555)
  - **Research question:** How does “Provable Adversarial Robustness for Group Equivariant Tasks: Graphs, Point Clouds, Molecules, and More” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** For the first time, the authors propose a sound notion of adversarial robustness that accounts for task equivariance.
- [What Distributions are Robust to Indiscriminate Poisoning Attacks for Linear Learners?](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/69920)
  - **Research question:** How does “What Distributions are Robust to Indiscriminate Poisoning Attacks for Linear Learners” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors study indiscriminate poisoning for linear learners where an adversary injects a few crafted examples into the training data with the goal of forcing the induced model to incur higher test error.
- [Maximization of Average Precision for Deep Learning with Adversarial Ranking Robustness](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70044)
  - **Research question:** How does “Maximization of Average Precision for Deep Learning with Adversarial Ranking Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To tackle this issue, the authors introduce a novel formulation that combines an AP surrogate loss with a regularization term representing adversarial ranking robustness, which maintains the consistency between ranking of clean data and that of perturbed data.
- [HQA-Attack: Toward High Quality Black-Box Hard-Label Adversarial Attack on Text](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72122)
  - **Research question:** How does “HQA-Attack: Toward High Quality Black-Box Hard-Label Adversarial Attack on Text” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To alleviate above issues, the authors propose a simple yet effective framework to generate high quality textual adversarial examples under the black-box hard-label attack scenarios, named HQA-Attack.
- [DFRD: Data-Free Robustness Distillation for Heterogeneous Federated Learning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72906)
  - **Research question:** How does “DFRD: Data-Free Robustness Distillation for Heterogeneous Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Additionally, the authors propose dynamic weighting and label sampling to accurately extract knowledge from local models.
- [Adversarial Robustness in Graph Neural Networks: A Hamiltonian Approach](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/69981)
  - **Research question:** How does “Adversarial Robustness in Graph Neural Networks: A Hamiltonian Approach” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate GNNs derived from diverse neural flows, concentrating on their connection to various stability notions such as BIBO stability, Lyapunov stability, structural stability, and conservative stability.
- [RECESS Vaccine for Federated Learning: Proactive Defense Against Model Poisoning Attacks](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72885)
  - **Research question:** How does “RECESS Vaccine for Federated Learning: Proactive Defense Against Model Poisoning Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel defense including detection and aggregation, named RECESS, to serve as a “vaccine” for FL against model poisoning attacks.
- [Wasserstein distributional robustness of neural networks](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71998)
  - **Research question:** How does “Wasserstein distributional robustness of neural networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To evaluate the distributional robustness of neural networks, the authors propose a first-order AA algorithm and its multistep version.
- [Enhancing Adversarial Robustness via Score-Based Optimization](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71912)
  - **Research question:** How does “Enhancing Adversarial Robustness via Score-Based Optimization” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a novel adversarial defense scheme named ScoreOpt, which optimizes adversarial samples at test-time, towards original clean data in the direction guided by score-based priors.
- [Robust Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining against Data Poisoning and Backdoor Attacks](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71818)
  - **Research question:** How does “Robust Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining against Data Poisoning and Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose RoCLIP, the first effective method for robust pre-training multimodal vision-language models against targeted data poisoning and backdoor attacks.
- [CBD: A Certified Backdoor Detector Based on Local Dominant Probability](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72180)
  - **Research question:** How does “CBD: A Certified Backdoor Detector Based on Local Dominant Probability” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present the first certified backdoor detector (CBD), which is based on a novel, adjustable conformal prediction scheme based on their proposed statistic local dominant probability.
- [A Unified Detection Framework for Inference-Stage Backdoor Defenses](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72827)
  - **Research question:** How does “A Unified Detection Framework for Inference-Stage Backdoor Defenses” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors devise a unified inference-stage detection framework to defend against backdoor attacks.
- [Content-based Unrestricted Adversarial Attack](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70854)
  - **Research question:** How does “Content-based Unrestricted Adversarial Attack” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To ensure the photorealism of adversarial examples and boost attack performance, the authors propose a novel unrestricted attack framework called Content-based Unrestricted Adversarial Attack.
- [Black-box Backdoor Defense via Zero-shot Image Purification](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71421)
  - **Research question:** How does “Black-box Backdoor Defense via Zero-shot Image Purification” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel defense framework against backdoor attacks through Zero-shot Image Purification (ZIP).
- [Asymmetric Certified Robustness via Feature-Convex Neural Networks](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71158)
  - **Research question:** How does “Asymmetric Certified Robustness via Feature-Convex Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors formalize the asymmetric robustness certification problem and correspondingly present the feature-convex neural network architecture, which composes an input-convex neural network (ICNN) with a Lipschitz continuous feature map in order to achieve asymmetric adversarial robustness.
- [Neural Polarizer: A Lightweight and Effective Backdoor Defense via Purifying Poisoned Features](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71467)
  - **Research question:** How does “Neural Polarizer: A Lightweight and Effective Backdoor Defense via Purifying Poisoned Features” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors insert a lightweight learnable linear layer into a backdoored model and train it with bilevel optimization to filter trigger information while retaining benign features.
- [One Less Reason for Filter Pruning: Gaining Free Adversarial Robustness with Structured Grouped Kernel Pruning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71747)
  - **Research question:** How does “One Less Reason for Filter Pruning: Gaining Free Adversarial Robustness with Structured Grouped Kernel Pruning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** By mixing up kernel smoothness — a classic robustness-related kernel-level metric — into a modified GKP procedure, the authors present a one-shot-post-train-weight-dependent GKP method capable of advancing SOTA performance on both the benign and adversarial scale, while requiring no extra (in fact, often less) cost than a standard pruning procedure.
- [Evaluating Post-hoc Explanations for Graph Neural Networks via Robustness Analysis](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70975)
  - **Research question:** How does “Evaluating Post-hoc Explanations for Graph Neural Networks via Robustness Analysis” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** For applications involving large datasets, the authors further devise a Sim plified version of OAR (SimOAR), which achieves a significant improvement in computational efficiency at the cost of a small amount of performance.
- [A3FL: Adversarially Adaptive Backdoor Attacks to Federated Learning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71628)
  - **Research question:** How does “A3FL: Adversarially Adaptive Backdoor Attacks to Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address these limitations, the authors propose A3FL, a new backdoor attack which adversarially adapts the backdoor trigger to make it less likely to be removed by the global training dynamics.
- [Revisiting Adversarial Robustness Distillation from the Perspective of Robust Fairness](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/69921)
  - **Research question:** How does “Revisiting Adversarial Robustness Distillation from the Perspective of Robust Fairness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors first investigate the inheritance of robust fairness during ARD and reveal that student models only partially inherit robust fairness from teacher models.
- [VillanDiffusion: A Unified Backdoor Attack Framework for Diffusion Models](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70045)
  - **Research question:** How does “VillanDiffusion: A Unified Backdoor Attack Framework for Diffusion Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a unified backdoor attack framework (VillanDiffusion) to expand the current scope of backdoor analysis for DMs.
- [Fed-FA: Theoretically Modeling Client Data Divergence for Federated Language Backdoor Defense](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70177)
  - **Research question:** How does “Fed-FA: Theoretically Modeling Client Data Divergence for Federated Language Backdoor Defense” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Furthermore, the authors devise a dataset synthesization method with a Hessian reassignment mechanism guided by the diffusion theory to address the key challenge of inaccessible datasets in calculating clients' data Hessians.
- [Improving Adversarial Robustness via Information Bottleneck Distillation](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70122)
  - **Research question:** How does “Improving Adversarial Robustness via Information Bottleneck Distillation” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive experiments to evaluate their approach's robustness against state-of-the-art adversarial attackers such as PGD-attack and AutoAttack.
- [On the Adversarial Robustness of Out-of-distribution Generalization Models](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72104)
  - **Research question:** How does “On the Adversarial Robustness of Out-of-distribution Generalization Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors first present theoretical analyses of OOD adversarial robustness in two different complementary settings.
- [Transferable Adversarial Robustness for Categorical Data via Universal Robust Embeddings](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71993)
  - **Research question:** How does “Transferable Adversarial Robustness for Categorical Data via Universal Robust Embeddings” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that their methods outperform existing techniques within a practical threat model suitable for tabular data.
- [CRoSS: Diffusion Model Makes Controllable, Robust and Secure Image Steganography](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72858)
  - **Research question:** How does “CRoSS: Diffusion Model Makes Controllable, Robust and Secure Image Steganography” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** In summary, the authors propose a novel image steganography framework, named Controllable, Robust and Secure Image Steganography (CRoSS), which has significant advantages in controllability, robustness, and security compared to cover-based image steganography methods.
- [Temporal Robustness against Data poisoning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71780)
  - **Research question:** How does “Temporal Robustness against Data poisoning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Benefiting from these timestamps, the authors propose a temporal threat model of data poisoning with two novel metrics, earliness and duration, which respectively measure how long an attack started in advance and how long an attack lasted.
- [Towards Stable Backdoor Purification through Feature Shift Tuning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72630)
  - **Research question:** How does “Towards Stable Backdoor Purification through Feature Shift Tuning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors introduce Feature Shift Tuning (FST), a method for tuning-based backdoor purification.

  - **What they did:** To address this issue, the authors propose BIRD, a technique to detect and remove backdoors from a pretrained DRL policy in a clean environment without requiring any knowledge about the attack specifications and accessing its training process.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [CoPriv: Network/Protocol Co-Optimization for Communication-Efficient Private Inference](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70354)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “CoPriv: Network/Protocol Co-Optimization for Communication-Efficient Private Inference” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present CoPriv, a framework that jointly optimizes the 2PC inference protocol and the DNN architecture.
- [A Privacy-Friendly Approach to Data Valuation](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72277)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Privacy-Friendly Approach to Data Valuation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To overcome these challenges, the authors introduce TKNN-Shapley, a refined variant of KNN-Shapley that is privacy-friendly, allowing for straightforward modifications to incorporate DP guarantee (DP-TKNN-Shapley).
- [Initialization Matters: Privacy-Utility Analysis of Overparameterized Neural Networks](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72128)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Initialization Matters: Privacy-Utility Analysis of Overparameterized Neural Networks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** By using this analysis, the authors demonstrate that privacy bound improves with increasing depth under certain initializations (LeCun and Xavier), while degrades with increasing depth under other initializations (He and NTK).
- [Posthoc privacy guarantees for collaborative inference with modified Propose-Test-Release](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72912)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Posthoc privacy guarantees for collaborative inference with modified Propose-Test-Release” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop a new framework that provides formal privacy guarantees for an arbitrarily trained neural network by linking its local Lipschitz constant with its local sensitivity.
- [k-Median Clustering via Metric Embedding: Towards Better Initialization with Differential Privacy](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72566)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “k-Median Clustering via Metric Embedding: Towards Better Initialization with Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new initialization scheme for the $k$-median problem in the general metric space (e.g., discrete space induced by graphs), based on the construction of metric embedding tree structure of the data.
- [Towards Unbounded Machine Unlearning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71789)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Towards Unbounded Machine Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** For UP, the authors propose a novel adaptation of a strong Membership Inference Attack for unlearning.
- [Emergent and Predictable Memorization in Large Language Models](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72096)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Emergent and Predictable Memorization in Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors measure memorization in the Pythia model suite and plot scaling laws for forecasting memorization, allowing us to provide equi-compute recommendations to maximize the reliability (recall) of such predictions.
- [Optimal Unbiased Randomizers for Regression with Label Differential Privacy](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71205)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Optimal Unbiased Randomizers for Regression with Label Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new family of label randomizers for training regression models under the constraint of label differential privacy (DP).
- [Certified Minimax Unlearning with Generalization Rates and Deletion Capacity](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72765)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Certified Minimax Unlearning with Generalization Rates and Deletion Capacity” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the problem of $(\epsilon,\delta)$-certified machine unlearning for minimax models.
- [User-Level Differential Privacy With Few Examples Per User](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71772)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “User-Level Differential Privacy With Few Examples Per User” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors transform item-level private algorithms into user-level algorithms for approximate differential privacy and adapt the exponential mechanism for pure privacy when each user supplies few examples.
- [GAN You See Me? Enhanced Data Reconstruction Attacks against Split Inference](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71290)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “GAN You See Me? Enhanced Data Reconstruction Attacks against Split Inference” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a GAN-based LAtent Space Search attack (GLASS) that harnesses abundant prior knowledge from public data using advanced StyleGAN technologies.
- [A Randomized Approach to Tight Privacy Accounting](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71330)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Randomized Approach to Tight Privacy Accounting” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new differential privacy paradigm called estimate-verify-release (EVR), which tackles the challenges of providing a strict upper bound for the privacy parameter in DP compositions by converting an *estimate* of privacy parameter into a formal guarantee.
- [Bounding training data reconstruction in DP-SGD](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72711)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Bounding training data reconstruction in DP-SGD” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate this question further in the context of DP-SGD, a standard algorithm for private deep learning, and provide an upper bound on the success of any reconstruction attack against DP-SGD together with an attack that empirically matches the predictions of their bound.
- [A Unified Solution for Privacy and Communication Efficiency in Vertical Federated Learning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72535)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Unified Solution for Privacy and Communication Efficiency in Vertical Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel cascaded hybrid optimization approach that employs a zeroth-order (ZO) gradient on the most critical output layer of the clients, with other parts utilizing the first-order (FO) gradient.
- [Gaussian Membership Inference Privacy](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72958)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Gaussian Membership Inference Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their method on models trained on vision and tabular datasets.
- [On the Gini-impurity Preservation For Privacy Random Forests](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71051)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “On the Gini-impurity Preservation For Privacy Random Forests” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive experiments to show the effectiveness, efficiency and security of their proposed method.
- [Incentives in Private Collaborative Machine Learning](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71365)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Incentives in Private Collaborative Machine Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors introduce differential privacy (DP) as an incentive.
- [Scalable Membership Inference Attacks via Quantile Regression](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70232)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Scalable Membership Inference Attacks via Quantile Regression” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that their method is competitive with state-of-the-art shadow model attacks, while requiring substantially less compute because their attack requires training only a single model.
