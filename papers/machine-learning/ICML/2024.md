# ICML 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [ICML 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/papers.html) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [COLD-Attack: Jailbreaking LLMs with Stealthiness and Controllability](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/32666)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “COLD-Attack: Jailbreaking LLMs with Stealthiness and Controllability” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors formally formulate the controllable attack generation problem, and build a novel connection between this problem and controllable text generation, a well-explored topic of natural language processing.
- [From Yes-Men to Truth-Tellers: Addressing Sycophancy in Large Language Models with Pinpoint Tuning](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33570)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “From Yes-Men to Truth-Tellers: Addressing Sycophancy in Large Language Models with Pinpoint Tuning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address the challenge, the authors propose a novel supervised pinpoint tuning (SPT), where the region-of-interest modules are tuned for a given objective.
- [HarmBench: A Standardized Evaluation Framework for Automated Red Teaming and Robust Refusal](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33475)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “HarmBench: A Standardized Evaluation Framework for Automated Red Teaming and Robust Refusal” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this issue, the authors introduce HarmBench, a standardized evaluation framework for automated red teaming.
- [Learning and Forgetting Unsafe Examples in Large Language Models](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34049)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Learning and Forgetting Unsafe Examples in Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Drawing inspiration from the discrepancies in forgetting, the authors introduce the “ForgetFilter” algorithm, which filters unsafe data based on how strong the model's forgetting signal is for that data.
- [Covert Malicious Finetuning: Challenges in Safeguarding LLM Adaptation](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34921)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Covert Malicious Finetuning: Challenges in Safeguarding LLM Adaptation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To demonstrate the challenge of defending finetuning interfaces, the authors introduce covert malicious finetuning, a method to compromise model safety via finetuning while evading detection.
- [Agent Smith: A Single Image Can Jailbreak One Million Multimodal LLM Agents Exponentially Fast](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34623)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Agent Smith: A Single Image Can Jailbreak One Million Multimodal LLM Agents Exponentially Fast” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Finally, the authors derive a simple principle for determining whether a defense mechanism can provably restrain the spread of infectious jailbreak, but how to design a practical defense that meets this principle remains an open question to investigate.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Adversarial Robustness Limits via Scaling-Law and Human-Alignment Studies](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34464)
  - **Research question:** How does “Adversarial Robustness Limits via Scaling-Law and Human-Alignment Studies” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To understand this gap, the authors analyze how model size, dataset size, and synthetic data quality affect robustness by developing the first scaling laws for adversarial training.
- [Naive Bayes Classifiers over Missing Data: Decision and Poisoning](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34069)
  - **Research question:** How does “Naive Bayes Classifiers over Missing Data: Decision and Poisoning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the certifiable robustness of ML classifiers on dirty datasets that could contain missing values.
- [IBD-PSC: Input-level Backdoor Detection via Parameter-oriented Scaling Consistency](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33779)
  - **Research question:** How does “IBD-PSC: Input-level Backdoor Detection via Parameter-oriented Scaling Consistency” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors also design an adaptive method to select BN layers to scale up for effective detection.
- [Data Poisoning Attacks against Conformal Prediction](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33473)
  - **Research question:** How does “Data Poisoning Attacks against Conformal Prediction” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Further, the authors conduct extensive experiments to validate the effectiveness of their attacks on various settings (e.g., the full and split CP settings).
- [OODRobustBench: a Benchmark and Large-Scale Analysis of Adversarial Robustness under Distribution Shift](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33270)
  - **Research question:** How does “OODRobustBench: a Benchmark and Large-Scale Analysis of Adversarial Robustness under Distribution Shift” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this issue the authors propose a benchmark named OODRobustBench to comprehensively assess OOD adversarial robustness using 23 dataset-wise shifts (i.e. naturalistic shifts in input distribution) and 6 threat-wise shifts (i.e., unforeseen adversarial threat models).
- [Causality Based Front-door Defense Against Backdoor Attack on Language Models](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33536)
  - **Research question:** How does “Causality Based Front-door Defense Against Backdoor Attack on Language Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new defense framework F ront-door A djustment for B ackdoor E limination (FABE) based on causal reasoning that does not rely on assumptions about the form of triggers.
- [Can Implicit Bias Imply Adversarial Robustness?](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34498)
  - **Research question:** How does “Can Implicit Bias Imply Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors extend recent analyses of neuron alignment to show that a shallow network with a polynomial ReLU activation (pReLU) trained by gradient flow not only generalizes well but is also robust to adversarial attacks.
- [IOI: Invisible One-Iteration Adversarial Attack on No-Reference Image- and Video-Quality Metrics](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34667)
  - **Research question:** How does “IOI: Invisible One-Iteration Adversarial Attack on No-Reference Image- and Video-Quality Metrics” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce an Invisible One-Iteration (IOI) adversarial attack on no-reference image and video quality metrics.
- [Exploration by Optimization with Hybrid Regularizers: Logarithmic Regret with Adversarial Robustness in Partial Monitoring](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33534)
  - **Research question:** How does “Exploration by Optimization with Hybrid Regularizers: Logarithmic Regret with Adversarial Robustness in Partial Monitoring” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To resolve this issue in locally observable games, the authors first establish a new framework and analysis for ExO with a hybrid regularizer.
- [FedREDefense: Defending against Model Poisoning Attacks for Federated Learning using Model Update Reconstruction Error](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33854)
  - **Research question:** How does “FedREDefense: Defending against Model Poisoning Attacks for Federated Learning using Model Update Reconstruction Error” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address these challenges, the authors present FedREDefense.
- [Better Safe than Sorry: Pre-training CLIP against Targeted Data Poisoning and Backdoor Attacks](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/32662)
  - **Research question:** How does “Better Safe than Sorry: Pre-training CLIP against Targeted Data Poisoning and Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a strong defense, SAFECLIP, to safely pre-train CLIP against targeted data poisoning and backdoor attacks.
- [Generalization Bound and New Algorithm for Clean-Label Backdoor Attack](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33728)
  - **Research question:** How does “Generalization Bound and New Algorithm for Clean-Label Backdoor Attack” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show its effectiveness in a variety of settings.
- [Rethinking Adversarial Robustness in the Context of the Right to be Forgotten](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/32857)
  - **Research question:** How does “Rethinking Adversarial Robustness in the Context of the Right to be Forgotten” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To exploit this observed vulnerability, the authors propose a novel attack called Adversarial Unlearning Attack (AdvUA), which aims to generate a small fraction of malicious unlearning requests during the unlearning process.
- [RAUCA: A Novel Physical Adversarial Attack on Vehicle Detectors via Robust and Accurate Camouflage Generation](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33051)
  - **Research question:** How does “RAUCA: A Novel Physical Adversarial Attack on Vehicle Detectors via Robust and Accurate Camouflage Generation” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To tackle these challenges, the authors propose a robust and accurate camouflage generation method, namely RAUCA.
- [A Theoretical Analysis of Backdoor Poisoning Attacks in Convolutional Neural Networks](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33997)
  - **Research question:** How does “A Theoretical Analysis of Backdoor Poisoning Attacks in Convolutional Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To fill this gap, the authors employ a dirty-label attack and conduct a detailed analysis of BPAs in a two-layer convolutional neural network.
- [Enhancing Adversarial Robustness in SNNs with Sparse Gradients](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34066)
  - **Research question:** How does “Enhancing Adversarial Robustness in SNNs with Sparse Gradients” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel approach to enhance the robustness of SNNs through gradient sparsity regularization.
- [Energy-based Backdoor Defense without Task-Specific Samples and Model Retraining](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33978)
  - **Research question:** How does “Energy-based Backdoor Defense without Task-Specific Samples and Model Retraining” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose two Energy-Based BAckdoor defense methods, called EBBA and EBBA+, that can achieve both backdoored model detection and backdoor removal with low overhead.
- [Attack-free Evaluating and Enhancing Adversarial Robustness on Categorical Data](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34854)
  - **Research question:** How does “Attack-free Evaluating and Enhancing Adversarial Robustness on Categorical Data” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a robustness evaluation metric named Integrated Gradient-Smoothed Gradient (IGSG).
- [CosPGD: an efficient white-box adversarial attack for pixel-wise prediction tasks](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34678)
  - **Research question:** How does “CosPGD: an efficient white-box adversarial attack for pixel-wise prediction tasks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Here, the authors propose CosPGD, an attack that encourages more balanced errors over the entire image domain while increasing the attack's overall efficiency.
- [Defense against Backdoor Attack on Pre-trained Language Models via Head Pruning and Attention Normalization](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/35148)
  - **Research question:** How does “Defense against Backdoor Attack on Pre-trained Language Models via Head Pruning and Attention Normalization” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To tackle this challenge, in this work, the authors propose a backdoor mitigation approach, PURE, via head pruning and normalization of attention weights.
- [Two Heads are Actually Better than One: Towards Better Adversarial Robustness via Transduction and Rejection](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/32772)
  - **Research question:** How does “Two Heads are Actually Better than One: Towards Better Adversarial Robustness via Transduction and Rejection” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Theoretically, the authors show that a careful application of this technique in the transductive setting can give significantly improved sample-complexity for robust generalization.

  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Low-Cost High-Power Membership Inference Attacks” investigate?

## 6. AI Safety, Alignment, and Responsible AI

- [Scalable AI Safety via Doubly-Efficient Debate](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34905)
  - **Research question:** How does “Scalable AI Safety via Doubly-Efficient Debate” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors show how to address these challenges by designing a new set of debate protocols where the honest strategy can always succeed using a simulation of a polynomial number of steps, whilst being able to verify the alignment of stochastic AI systems, even when the dishonest strategy is allowed to use exponentially many simulation steps.
- [AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34322)
  - **Research question:** How does “AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop and evaluate pipelines of safety techniques (protocols) that try to ensure safety despite intentional subversion - an approach the authors call AI control.
- [Position: A Safe Harbor for AI Evaluation and Red Teaming](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/33560)
  - **Research question:** How does “Position: A Safe Harbor for AI Evaluation and Red Teaming” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose that major generative AI developers commit to providing a legal and technical safe harbor, protecting public interest safety research and removing the threat of account suspensions or legal reprisal.
- [Assessing the Brittleness of Safety Alignment via Pruning and Low-Rank Modifications](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34354)
  - **Research question:** How does “Assessing the Brittleness of Safety Alignment via Pruning and Low-Rank Modifications” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop methods to identify critical regions that are vital for safety guardrails, and that are disentangled from utility-relevant regions at both the neuron and rank levels.
