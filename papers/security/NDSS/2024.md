# NDSS 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [NDSS 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss2024/accepted-papers/) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [Large Language Model guided Protocol Fuzzing](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/large-language-model-guided-protocol-fuzzing/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Large Language Model guided Protocol Fuzzing” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors also checked the LLM's capability in detecting “states" for stateful protocol implementations by generating sequences of messages and predicting response codes.
- [MASTERKEY: Automated Jailbreaking of Large Language Model Chatbots](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/masterkey-automated-jailbreaking-of-large-language-model-chatbots/)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “MASTERKEY: Automated Jailbreaking of Large Language Model Chatbots” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel method based on time-based characteristics to dissect LLM chatbot defenses.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Automatic Adversarial Adaption for Stealthy Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/automatic-adversarial-adaption-for-stealthy-poisoning-attacks-in-federated-learning/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Automatic Adversarial Adaption for Stealthy Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose AutoAdapt, a novel adaptation method that leverages an Augmented Lagrangian optimization technique.
- [CrowdGuard: Federated Backdoor Detection in Federated Learning](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/crowdguard-federated-backdoor-detection-in-federated-learning/)
  - **Research question:** How does “CrowdGuard: Federated Backdoor Detection in Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel defense mechanism, CrowdGuard, that effectively mitigates backdoor attacks in FL and overcomes the deficiencies of existing techniques.
- [FreqFed: A Frequency Analysis-Based Approach for Mitigating Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/freqfed-a-frequency-analysis-based-approach-for-mitigating-poisoning-attacks-in-federated-learning/)
  - **Research question:** How does “FreqFed: A Frequency Analysis-Based Approach for Mitigating Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present FreqFed, a novel aggregation mechanism that transforms the model updates (i.e., weights) into the frequency domain, where the authors can identify the core frequency components that inherit sufficient information about weights.
- [Gradient Shaping: Enhancing Backdoor Attack Against Reverse Engineering](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/gradient-shaping-enhancing-backdoor-attack-against-reverse-engineering/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Gradient Shaping: Enhancing Backdoor Attack Against Reverse Engineering” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** In the meantime, the authors found that the low change rate is not necessary for a backdoor attack to succeed: the authors design a new attack enhancement method called Gradient Shaping (GRASP), which follows the opposite direction of adversarial training to reduce the change rate of a backdoored model with regard to the trigger, without undermining its backdoor effect.
- [Sneaky Spikes: Uncovering Stealthy Backdoor Attacks in Spiking Neural Networks with Neuromorphic Data](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/sneaky-spikes-uncovering-stealthy-backdoor-attacks-in-spiking-neural-networks-with-neuromorphic-data/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Sneaky Spikes: Uncovering Stealthy Backdoor Attacks in Spiking Neural Networks with Neuromorphic Data” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present various attack strategies, achieving an attack success rate of up to 100% while maintaining a negligible impact on clean accuracy.
- [TextGuard: Provable Defense against Backdoor Attacks on Text Classification](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/textguard-provable-defense-against-backdoor-attacks-on-text-classification/)
  - **Research question:** How does “TextGuard: Provable Defense against Backdoor Attacks on Text Classification” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose TextGuard, the first provable defense against backdoor attacks on text classification.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [A Duty to Forget, a Right to be Assured? Exposing Vulnerabilities in Machine Unlearning Services](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/a-duty-to-forget-a-right-to-be-assured-exposing-vulnerabilities-in-machine-unlearning-services/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Duty to Forget, a Right to be Assured? Exposing Vulnerabilities in Machine Unlearning Services” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose two strategies that leverage over-unlearning to measure the impact on the trade-off balancing, under black-box access settings, in which the existing machine unlearning attacks are not applicable.
- [FP-Fed: Privacy-Preserving Federated Detection of Browser Fingerprinting](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/fp-fed-privacy-preserving-federated-detection-of-browser-fingerprinting/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “FP-Fed: Privacy-Preserving Federated Detection of Browser Fingerprinting” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these limitations, the authors present FP-Fed, the first distributed system for browser fingerprinting detection.
- [Overconfidence is a Dangerous Thing: Mitigating Membership Inference Attacks by Enforcing Less Confident Prediction](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/overconfidence-is-a-dangerous-thing-mitigating-membership-inference-attacks-by-enforcing-less-confident-prediction/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Overconfidence is a Dangerous Thing: Mitigating Membership Inference Attacks by Enforcing Less Confident Prediction” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive evaluation on five benchmark datasets, and show that HAMP provides consistently high accuracy and strong membership privacy.
- [SLMIA-SR: Speaker-Level Membership Inference Attacks against Speaker Recognition Systems](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/slmia-sr-speaker-level-membership-inference-attacks-against-speaker-recognition-systems/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SLMIA-SR: Speaker-Level Membership Inference Attacks against Speaker Recognition Systems” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To improve the generalizability of their attack, the authors propose a novel mixing ratio training strategy to train attack models.
- [You Can Use But Cannot Recognize: Preserving Visual Privacy in Deep Neural Networks](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/you-can-use-but-cannot-recognize-preserving-visual-privacy-in-deep-neural-networks/)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “You Can Use But Cannot Recognize: Preserving Visual Privacy in Deep Neural Networks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel privacy-preserving framework VisualMixer that protects the training data of visual DNN tasks by pixel shuffling, while not injecting any noises.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [Detecting Voice Cloning Attacks via Timbre Watermarking](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/detecting-voice-cloning-attacks-via-timbre-watermarking/)
  - **Research question:** How does “Detecting Voice Cloning Attacks via Timbre Watermarking” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors introduce a novel concept, "Timbre Watermarking", which embeds watermark information into the target individual's speech, eventually defeating the voice cloning attacks.
- [SSL-WM: A Black-Box Watermarking Approach for Encoders Pre-trained by Self-Supervised Learning](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/ssl-wm-a-black-box-watermarking-approach-for-encoders-pre-trained-by-self-supervised-learning/)
  - **Research question:** How does “SSL-WM: A Black-Box Watermarking Approach for Encoders Pre-trained by Self-Supervised Learning” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To address such a problem, especially when downstream tasks are diverse and unknown during watermark embedding, the authors propose a novel black-box watermarking solution, named SSL-WM, for verifying the ownership of SSL models.

## 5. AI Safety, Alignment, and Responsible AI

- [GraphGuard: Detecting and Counteracting Training Data Misuse in Graph Neural Networks](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/graphguard-detecting-and-counteracting-training-data-misuse-in-graph-neural-networks/)
  - **Research question:** How does “GraphGuard: Detecting and Counteracting Training Data Misuse in Graph Neural Networks” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a training-data-free method that not only detects graph data misuse but also mitigates its impact via targeted unlearning, all without relying on the original training data.
