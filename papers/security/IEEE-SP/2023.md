# IEEE S&P 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [IEEE S&P 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://sp2023.ieee-security.org/program-papers.html) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [RoFL: Robustness of Secure Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179400)
  - **Research question:** How does “RoFL: Robustness of Secure Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that the need for ML algorithms to memorize tail data has significant implications for FL integrity.
- [Selective Amnesia: On Efficient, High-Fidelity and Blind Suppression of Backdoor Effects in Trojaned Machine Learning Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10351028)
  - **Research question:** How does “Selective Amnesia: On Efficient, High-Fidelity and Blind Suppression of Backdoor Effects in Trojaned Machine Learning Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a simple yet surprisingly effective technique to induce "selective amnesia" on a backdoored model.
- [Jigsaw Puzzle: Selective Backdoor Attack to Subvert Malware Classifiers](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179347)
  - **Research question:** How does “Jigsaw Puzzle: Selective Backdoor Attack to Subvert Malware Classifiers” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Empirically, the authors show that existing backdoor attacks against malware classifiers are still detectable by recent defenses such as MNTD.
- [BayBFed: Bayesian Backdoor Defense for Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179362)
  - **Research question:** How does “BayBFed: Bayesian Backdoor Defense for Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose BayBFed, which models distributions over client updates and uses Bayesian nonparametric processes to detect and filter malicious federated-learning updates.
- [SoK: Certified Robustness for Deep Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179303)
  - **Research question:** How does “SoK: Certified Robustness for Deep Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors also provide the first comprehensive benchmark on existing robustness verification and training approaches on different datasets.
- [RAB: Provable Robustness Against Backdoor Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179451)
  - **Research question:** How does “RAB: Provable Robustness Against Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** In addition, the authors theoretically show that it is possible to train the robust smoothed models efficiently for simple models such as K-nearest neighbor classifiers, and the authors propose an exact smooth-training algorithm that eliminates the need to sample from a noise distribution for such models.
- [FedRecover: Recovering from Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning using Historical Information](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179336)
  - **Research question:** How does “FedRecover: Recovering from Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning using Historical Information” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose FedRecover, a method that can recover an accurate global model from poisoning attacks with a small computation and communication cost for the clients.
- [TrojanModel: A Practical Trojan Attack against Automatic Speech Recognition Systems](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179331)
  - **Research question:** How does “TrojanModel: A Practical Trojan Attack against Automatic Speech Recognition Systems” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present TrojanModel, a practical Trojan attack against Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems.
- [3DFed: Adaptive and Extensible Framework for Covert Backdoor Attack in Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179401)
  - **Research question:** How does “3DFed: Adaptive and Extensible Framework for Covert Backdoor Attack in Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose 3DFed, an adaptive black-box federated-learning backdoor framework with three evasion modules and feedback-based hyperparameter adjustment.

## 2. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [SoK: Let the Privacy Games Begin! A Unified Treatment of Data Inference Privacy in Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179281)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SoK: Let the Privacy Games Begin! A Unified Treatment of Data Inference Privacy in Machine Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a game-based framework to systematize the body of knowledge on privacy inference risks in machine learning.
- [Accuracy-Privacy Trade-off in Deep Ensemble: A Membership Inference Perspective](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179463)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Accuracy-Privacy Trade-off in Deep Ensemble: A Membership Inference Perspective” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Using a wide range of datasets and model architectures, the authors show that the effectiveness of membership inference attacks increases when ensembling improves accuracy.
- [D-DAE: Defense-Penetrating Model Extraction Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179406)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “D-DAE: Defense-Penetrating Model Extraction Attacks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors design a meta-learning-based disruption detection algorithm for learning the fundamental differences between the distributions of disrupted and undisrupted query results.
- [SNAP: Efficient Extraction of Private Properties with Poisoning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179334)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SNAP: Efficient Extraction of Private Properties with Poisoning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluate their attack on several properties of varying proportions from four datasets and demonstrate SNAP’s generality and effectiveness.
- [Flamingo: Multi-Round Single-Server Secure Aggregation with Applications to Private Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179434)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Flamingo: Multi-Round Single-Server Secure Aggregation with Applications to Private Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors implement and evaluate Flamingo and show that it can securely train a neural network on the (Extended) MNIST and CIFAR-100 datasets, and the model converges without a loss in accuracy, compared to a non-private federated learning system.
- [Bicoptor: Two-round Secure Three-party Non-linear Computation without Preprocessing for Privacy-preserving Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179449)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Bicoptor: Two-round Secure Three-party Non-linear Computation without Preprocessing for Privacy-preserving Machine Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop suitable protocols for these non-linear functions, which form a family of GPU-friendly protocols, Bicoptor.
- [Scalable and Privacy-Preserving Federated Principal Component Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179350)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Scalable and Privacy-Preserving Federated Principal Component Analysis” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors address the problem of performing a federated PCA on private data distributed among multiple data providers while ensuring data confidentiality.
- [Private, Efficient, and Accurate: Protecting Models Trained by Multi-party Learning with Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179422)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Private, Efficient, and Accurate: Protecting Models Trained by Multi-party Learning with Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** First, the authors propose a secure DPSGD protocol to enforce DPSGD, which is a popular differentially private machine learning algorithm, in secret sharing-based MPL frameworks.
- [Spectral-DP: Differentially Private Deep Learning through Spectral Perturbation and Filtering](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179457)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Spectral-DP: Differentially Private Deep Learning through Spectral Perturbation and Filtering” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Spectral-DP, a new differentially private learning approach which combines gradient perturbation in the spectral domain with spectral filtering to achieve a desired privacy guarantee with a lower noise scale and thus better utility.

## 3. Secure and Verifiable AI Systems

- [ShadowNet: A Secure and Efficient On-device Model Inference System for Convolutional Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179382)
  - **Research question:** How does “ShadowNet: A Secure and Efficient On-device Model Inference System for Convolutional Neural Networks” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel on-device model inference system, ShadowNet.
- [ELSA: Secure Aggregation for Federated Learning with Malicious Actors](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179468)
  - **Research question:** How does “ELSA: Secure Aggregation for Federated Learning with Malicious Actors” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Elsa, a secure aggregation protocol for FL, which breaks this barrier - it is efficient and addresses the existence of malicious actors at the core of its design.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [Deepfake Text Detection: Limitations and Opportunities](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179387)
  - **Research question:** How does “Deepfake Text Detection: Limitations and Opportunities” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors collect deepfake text from 4 online services powered by Transformer-based tools to evaluate the generalization ability of the defenses on content in the wild.

## 5. Other AI Security and Privacy

- [On the (In)security of Peer-to-Peer Decentralized Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179291)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “On the (In)security of Peer-to-Peer Decentralized Machine Learning” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a suite of novel attacks for both passive and active decentralized adversaries.
