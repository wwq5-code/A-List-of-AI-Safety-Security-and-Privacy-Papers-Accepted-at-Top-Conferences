# IEEE S&P 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [IEEE S&P 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2024/accepted-papers.html) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [SneakyPrompt: Jailbreaking Text-to-image Generative Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00123)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “SneakyPrompt: Jailbreaking Text-to-image Generative Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose SneakyPrompt, the first automated attack framework, to jailbreak text-to-image generative models such that they generate NSFW images even if safety filters are adopted.
- [LLMs Cannot Reliably Identify and Reason About Security Vulnerabilities (Yet?): A Comprehensive Evaluation, Framework, and Benchmarks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00210)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “LLMs Cannot Reliably Identify and Reason About Security Vulnerabilities (Yet?): A Comprehensive Evaluation, Framework, and Benchmarks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors construct a set of 228 code scenarios and analyze eight of the most capable LLMs across eight different investigative dimensions using their framework.
- [LLMIF: Augmented Large Language Model for Fuzzing IoT Devices](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00211)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “LLMIF: Augmented Large Language Model for Fuzzing IoT Devices” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that the protocol specification contains fruitful descriptions of protocol messages, which can be used to overcome the above limitations and guide IoT protocol fuzzing.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Poisoning Web-Scale Training Datasets is Practical](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00179)
  - **Research question:** How does “Poisoning Web-Scale Training Datasets is Practical” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce two new dataset poisoning attacks that intentionally introduce malicious examples to a model’s performance.
- [FlowMur: A Stealthy and Practical Audio Backdoor Attack with Limited Knowledge](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00148)
  - **Research question:** How does “FlowMur: A Stealthy and Practical Audio Backdoor Attack with Limited Knowledge” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address these problems, in this paper, the authors propose FlowMur, a stealthy and practical audio backdoor attack that can be launched with limited knowledge.
- [Robust Backdoor Detection for Deep Learning via Topological Evolution Dynamics](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00174)
  - **Research question:** How does “Robust Backdoor Detection for Deep Learning via Topological Evolution Dynamics” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that this assumption has a severe limitation by introducing a novel SSDT (Source-Specific and Dynamic-Triggers) backdoor, which obscures the difference between normal samples and malicious samples.
- [TROJANPUZZLE: Covertly Poisoning Code-Suggestion Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00140)
  - **Research question:** How does “TROJANPUZZLE: Covertly Poisoning Code-Suggestion Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate two novel attacks, Covert and TrojanPuzzle, that can bypass static analysis by planting malicious poison data in out-of-context regions such as docstrings.
- [FLShield: A Validation Based Federated Learning Framework to Defend Against Poisoning Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00141)
  - **Research question:** How does “FLShield: A Validation Based Federated Learning Framework to Defend Against Poisoning Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel FL framework dubbed as FLShield that utilizes benign data from FL participants to validate the local models before taking them into account for generating the global model.
- [Rethinking IC Layout Vulnerability: Simulation-Based Hardware Trojan Threat Assessment with High Fidelity](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00160)
  - **Research question:** How does “Rethinking IC Layout Vulnerability: Simulation-Based Hardware Trojan Threat Assessment with High Fidelity” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To bridge the gap between real attacks and threat prediction, the authors present SiliconCritic, a simulation-based, extensible framework that leverages design-time techniques to simulate the blackbox foundry-level Trojan attacks and post-fabrication analysis.
- [ODSCAN: Backdoor Scanning for Object Detection Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00119)
  - **Research question:** How does “ODSCAN: Backdoor Scanning for Object Detection Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a new trigger inversion technique that leverages a number of critical observations to reduce the search space to an affordable level.
- [Poisoned ChatGPT Finds Work for Idle Hands: Exploring Developers' Coding Practices with Insecure Suggestions from Poisoned AI Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00046)
  - **Research question:** How does “Poisoned ChatGPT Finds Work for Idle Hands: Exploring Developers' Coding Practices with Insecure Suggestions from Poisoned AI Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To understand the real-world impact of poisoning attacks on developers who rely on AI-powered coding assistants, the authors conducted two user studies: an online survey and an in-lab study.
- [Nightshade: Prompt-Specific Poisoning Attacks on Text-to-Image Generative Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00207)
  - **Research question:** How does “Nightshade: Prompt-Specific Poisoning Attacks on Text-to-Image Generative Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that state-of-the-art text-to-image generative models are in fact highly vulnerable to poisoning attacks.
- [Need for Speed: Taming Backdoor Attacks with Speed and Precision](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00216)
  - **Research question:** How does “Need for Speed: Taming Backdoor Attacks with Speed and Precision” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a lightweight backdoor identification and removal scheme, called ReBack.
- [Exploring the Orthogonality and Linearity of Backdoor Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00225)
  - **Research question:** How does “Exploring the Orthogonality and Linearity of Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To understand the reason, the authors study the characteristics of backdoor attacks through theoretical analysis.
- [BELT: Old-School Backdoor Attacks can Evade the State-of-the-Art Defense with Backdoor Exclusivity Lifting](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00226)
  - **Research question:** How does “BELT: Old-School Backdoor Attacks can Evade the State-of-the-Art Defense with Backdoor Exclusivity Lifting” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose and investigate a new characteristic of backdoor attacks, namely, backdoor exclusivity, which measures the ability of backdoor triggers to remain effective in the presence of input variation.
- [BounceAttack: A Query-Efficient Decision-based Adversarial Attack by Bouncing into the Wild](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00068)
  - **Research question:** How does “BounceAttack: A Query-Efficient Decision-based Adversarial Attack by Bouncing into the Wild” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors study such threats in the decision-based black-box setting where the adversary could obtain only the predicted labels of the victim classifier within limited queries and aims at performing targeted and untargeted adversarial attacks under different perturbation constraints.
- [DeepShuffle: A Lightweight Defense Framework against Adversarial Fault Injection Attacks on Deep Neural Networks in Multi-Tenant Cloud-FPGA](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00034)
  - **Research question:** How does “DeepShuffle: A Lightweight Defense Framework against Adversarial Fault Injection Attacks on Deep Neural Networks in Multi-Tenant Cloud-FPGA” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluate DeepShuffle on the SOTA open-source FPGA-DNN accelerator, Vertical Tensor Accelerator (VTA), which represents the practice of real-world FPGA-DNN system developers.
- [MMBD: Post-Training Detection of Backdoor Attacks with Arbitrary Backdoor Pattern Types Using a Maximum Margin Statistic](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00015)
  - **Research question:** How does “MMBD: Post-Training Detection of Backdoor Attacks with Arbitrary Backdoor Pattern Types Using a Maximum Margin Statistic” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** In contrast, the authors propose a post-training defense that detects backdoor attacks with arbitrary types of backdoor embeddings, without making any assumptions about the backdoor embedding type.
- [Test-Time Poisoning Attacks Against Test-Time Adaptation Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00072)
  - **Research question:** How does “Test-Time Poisoning Attacks Against Test-Time Adaptation Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this challenge, several test-time adaptation (TTA) methods have been proposed to improve the generalization ability of the target pre-trained models under test data to cope with the shifted distribution.
- [BadVFL: Backdoor Attacks in Vertical Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00008)
  - **Research question:** How does “BadVFL: Backdoor Attacks in Vertical Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a first-of-its-kind clean-label backdoor attack in VFL, which consists of two phases: a label inference and a backdoor phase.
- [Multi-Instance Adversarial Attack on GNN-Based Malicious Domain Detection](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00006)
  - **Research question:** How does “Multi-Instance Adversarial Attack on GNN-Based Malicious Domain Detection” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present both theoretical and empirical evidence to show that the existing single-instance evasion techniques for GNN-based MDDs are inadequate to launch multi-instance evasion attacks.
- [Distribution Preserving Backdoor Attack in Self-supervised Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00029)
  - **Research question:** How does “Distribution Preserving Backdoor Attack in Self-supervised Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel distribution preserving attack, which transforms the poisoned samples into in-distribution data by reducing their distributional distance to the clean data.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Eureka: A General Framework for Black-box Differential Privacy Estimators](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00166)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Eureka: A General Framework for Black-box Differential Privacy Estimators” investigate?
  - **What they did:** First, using kNN as the classifier the authors show that their method (1) produces a tight, analytically computed (ε,δ)-DP trade-off of low-dimensional Laplace and Gaussian mechanisms—the first to do so, (2) accurately estimates the privacy spectrum of DDP mechanisms, and (3) can verify a DP mechanism's implementations, e.g., Sparse Vector Technique, Noisy Histogram, and Noisy max.
- [From Individual Computation to Allied Optimization: Remodeling Privacy-Preserving Neural Inference with Function Input Tuning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00101)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “From Individual Computation to Allied Optimization: Remodeling Privacy-Preserving Neural Inference with Function Input Tuning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** As such the authors propose FIT (Function Input Tuning) which features by a computation module for composite function with a series of joint optimization strategies.
- [Please Tell Me More: Privacy Impact of Explainability through the Lens of Membership Inference Attack](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00120)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Please Tell Me More: Privacy Impact of Explainability through the Lens of Membership Inference Attack” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluated their approach with seven popular explanators across various benchmark models and datasets.
- [MEA-Defender: A Robust Watermark against Model Extraction Attack](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00099)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “MEA-Defender: A Robust Watermark against Model Extraction Attack” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel watermark to protect IP of DNN models against model extraction, named MEA-Defender.
- [BOLT: Privacy-Preserving, Accurate and Efficient Inference for Transformers](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00130)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “BOLT: Privacy-Preserving, Accurate and Efficient Inference for Transformers” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present BOLT, a privacy-preserving inference framework for transformer models that supports efficient matrix multiplications and nonlinear computations.
- [Preserving Node-level Privacy in Graph Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00270)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Preserving Node-level Privacy in Graph Neural Networks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a solution that specifically addresses the issue of node-level privacy.
- [Learn What You Want to Unlearn: Unlearning Inversion Attacks against Machine Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00248)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Learn What You Want to Unlearn: Unlearning Inversion Attacks against Machine Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** As such, the authors identify three possible defenses that help to mitigate the proposed attacks, while at the cost of reducing the utility of the unlearned model.
- [SHERPA: Explainable Robust Algorithms for Privacy-preserved Federated Learning in Future Networks to Defend against Data Poisoning Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00271)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SHERPA: Explainable Robust Algorithms for Privacy-preserved Federated Learning in Future Networks to Defend against Data Poisoning Attacks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Furthermore, the authors show that privacy-targeted poisoning attacks can be mitigated with their approach.
- [Few-shot Unlearning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00249)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Few-shot Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate that their method using only a tiny subset of target data can achieve similar performance to the state-of-the-art methods with full access to target data.
- [No Privacy Left Outside: On the (In-)Security of TEE-Shielded DNN Partition for On-Device ML](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00052)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “No Privacy Left Outside: On the (In-)Security of TEE-Shielded DNN Partition for On-Device ML” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show important findings that existing TSDP solutions are vulnerable to privacy-stealing attacks and are not as safe as commonly believed.
- [LOKI: Large-scale Data Reconstruction Attack against Federated Learning through Model Manipulation](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00030)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “LOKI: Large-scale Data Reconstruction Attack against Federated Learning through Model Manipulation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce Loki, an attack that overcomes previous limitations and also breaks the anonymity of aggregation as the leaked data is identifiable and directly tied back to the clients they come from.
- [SoK: Privacy-Preserving Data Synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00002)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SoK: Privacy-Preserving Data Synthesis” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Finally, upon systematizing the work over the past decade, the authors identify future directions and call for actions from researchers.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [SrcMarker: Dual-Channel Source Code Watermarking via Scalable Code Transformations](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00097)
  - **Research question:** How does “SrcMarker: Dual-Channel Source Code Watermarking via Scalable Code Transformations” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** Hence the authors introduce SrcMarker, a watermarking system to unobtrusively encode ID bitstrings into source code, without affecting the usage and semantics of the code.
- [AVA: Inconspicuous Attribute Variation-based Adversarial Attack bypassing DeepFake Detection](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00155)
  - **Research question:** How does “AVA: Inconspicuous Attribute Variation-based Adversarial Attack bypassing DeepFake Detection” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluate their proposed AVA attack on nine state-of-the-art DeepFake detection algorithms and applications.
- [FLASH: A Comprehensive Approach to Intrusion Detection via Provenance Graph Representation Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00139)
  - **Research question:** How does “FLASH: A Comprehensive Approach to Intrusion Detection via Provenance Graph Representation Learning” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Flash, a scalable IDS that leverages graph representation learning through Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) on data provenance graphs to overcome these limitations.
- [PromptCARE: Prompt Copyright Protection by Watermark Injection and Verification](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00209)
  - **Research question:** How does “PromptCARE: Prompt Copyright Protection by Watermark Injection and Verification” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose PromptCARE, the first framework for prompt copyright protection through watermark injection and verification.
- [An Analysis of Recent Advances in Deepfake Image Detection in an Evolving Threat Landscape](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00194)
  - **Research question:** How does “An Analysis of Recent Advances in Deepfake Image Detection in an Evolving Threat Landscape” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors study 8 state-of-the-art detectors and argue that they are far from being ready for deployment due to two recent developments.
- [KAIROS: Practical Intrusion Detection and Investigation using Whole-system Provenance](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00005)
  - **Research question:** How does “KAIROS: Practical Intrusion Detection and Investigation using Whole-system Provenance” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Kairos, the first PIDS that simultaneously satisfies the desiderata in all four dimensions, whereas existing approaches sacrifice at least one and struggle to achieve comparable detection performance.
- [R-CAID: Embedding Root Cause Analysis within Provenance-based Intrusion Detection](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00253)
  - **Research question:** How does “R-CAID: Embedding Root Cause Analysis within Provenance-based Intrusion Detection” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors present the design and implementation of R-CAID, a novel approach to incorporate RCA into provenance-based IDS.

## 5. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [Universal Neural-Cracking-Machines: Self-Configurable Password Models from Auxiliary Data](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp54263.2024.00032)
  - **Research question:** How does “Universal Neural-Cracking-Machines: Self-Configurable Password Models from Auxiliary Data” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the concept of "universal" password model—a password model that, once pre-trained, can automatically adapt its guessing strategy based on the target system.
