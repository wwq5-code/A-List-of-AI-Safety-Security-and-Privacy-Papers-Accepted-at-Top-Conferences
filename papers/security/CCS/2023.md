# CCS 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [CCS 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://dl.acm.org/doi/proceedings/10.1145/3576915) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Narcissus: A Practical Clean-Label Backdoor Attack with Limited Information](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616617)
  - **Research question:** How does “Narcissus: A Practical Clean-Label Backdoor Attack with Limited Information” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors analyze the effectiveness of their attack - the synthesized Narcissus trigger contains durable features as persistent as the original target class features.
- [MDTD: A Multi-Domain Trojan Detector for Deep Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623082)
  - **Research question:** How does “MDTD: A Multi-Domain Trojan Detector for Deep Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose MDTD, a Multi-Domain Trojan Detector for DNNs, which detects inputs containing a Trojan trigger at testing time.
- [MESAS: Poisoning Defense for Federated Learning Resilient against Adaptive Attackers](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623212)
  - **Research question:** How does “MESAS: Poisoning Defense for Federated Learning Resilient against Adaptive Attackers” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Metric-Cascades (MESAS), a novel defense method tailored to more realistic scenarios and adversary models.

## 2. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Prediction Privacy in Distributed Multi-Exit Neural Networks: Vulnerabilities and Solutions](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623069)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Prediction Privacy in Distributed Multi-Exit Neural Networks: Vulnerabilities and Solutions” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a side-channel attack against distributed MeNNs employing data-dependent early exit policies.
- [Devil in Disguise: Breaching Graph Neural Networks Privacy through Infiltration](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623173)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Devil in Disguise: Breaching Graph Neural Networks Privacy through Infiltration” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose Infiltrator, a privacy attack that is able to pry node-level private information based on black-box access to GNNs.
- [DPMLBench: Holistic Evaluation of Differentially Private Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616593)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “DPMLBench: Holistic Evaluation of Differentially Private Machine Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors first present a taxonomy of where improvements are located in the ML life cycle.
- [Geometry of Sensitivity: Twice Sampling and Hybrid Clipping in Differential Privacy with Optimal Gaussian Noise and Application to Deep Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623142)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Geometry of Sensitivity: Twice Sampling and Hybrid Clipping in Differential Privacy with Optimal Gaussian Noise and Application to Deep Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the fundamental problem of the construction of optimal randomization in Differential Privacy (DP).
- [DP-Forward: Fine-tuning and Inference on Language Models with Differential Privacy in Forward Pass](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616592)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “DP-Forward: Fine-tuning and Inference on Language Models with Differential Privacy in Forward Pass” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose DP-Forward, which directly perturbs embedding matrices in the forward pass of LMs.
- [Unraveling the Connections between Privacy and Certified Robustness in Federated Learning Against Poisoning Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623193)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Unraveling the Connections between Privacy and Certified Robustness in Federated Learning Against Poisoning Attacks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors first investigate both user-level and instance-level privacy of FL and provide formal privacy analysis to achieve improved instance-level privacy.
- [Turning Privacy-preserving Mechanisms against Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623114)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Turning Privacy-preserving Mechanisms against Federated Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors identify a crucial security flaw in such a configuration and design an attack capable of deceiving state-of-the-art defenses for federated learning.
- [MicPro: Microphone-based Voice Privacy Protection](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616616)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “MicPro: Microphone-based Voice Privacy Protection” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors implement MicPro on an off-the-shelf microphone module and evaluate the performance of MicPro on several ASV systems, ASR systems, corpora, and in real-world setup.
- [Group and Attack: Auditing Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616607)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Group and Attack: Auditing Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors implemented their approach in a tool called Delta-Siege and demonstrated its effectiveness by discovering vulnerabilities in most of the evaluated frameworks, several of which were previously unknown.
- [Blink: Link Local Differential Privacy in Graph Neural Networks via Bayesian Estimation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623165)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Blink: Link Local Differential Privacy in Graph Neural Networks via Bayesian Estimation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors propose using link local differential privacy over decentralized nodes, enabling collaboration with an untrusted server to train GNNs without revealing the existence of any link.

## 3. Secure and Verifiable AI Systems

- [HELiKs: HE Linear Algebra Kernels for Secure Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623136)
  - **Research question:** How does “HELiKs: HE Linear Algebra Kernels for Secure Inference” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce HELiKs, a groundbreaking framework for fast and secure matrix multiplication and 3D convolutions, tailored for privacy-preserving machine learning.

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [Evading Watermark based Detection of AI-Generated Content](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623189)
  - **Research question:** How does “Evading Watermark based Detection of AI-Generated Content” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors show the effectiveness of their attack both theoretically and empirically.

## 5. AI Safety, Alignment, and Responsible AI

- [Unsafe Diffusion: On the Generation of Unsafe Images and Hateful Memes From Text-To-Image Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616679)
  - **Research question:** How does “Unsafe Diffusion: On the Generation of Unsafe Images and Hateful Memes From Text-To-Image Models” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors first construct a typology of unsafe images consisting of five categories (sexually explicit, violent, disturbing, hateful, and political).

## 6. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [Improving Security Tasks Using Compiler Provenance Information Recovered At the Binary-Level](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623098)
  - **Research question:** How does “Improving Security Tasks Using Compiler Provenance Information Recovered At the Binary-Level” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors train a multi-label compiler-provenance model on more than 27,000 programs and apply its recovered provenance to vulnerability discovery, forensic checks, and binary decomposition.
- [Decoding the Secrets of Machine Learning in Malware Classification: A Deep Dive into Datasets, Feature Extraction, and Model Performance](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616589)
  - **Research question:** How does “Decoding the Secrets of Machine Learning in Malware Classification: A Deep Dive into Datasets, Feature Extraction, and Model Performance” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** For this, the authors collect the largest balanced malware dataset so far with 67k samples from 670 families (100 samples each), and train state-of-the-art models for malware detection and family classification using their dataset.
- [SalsaPicante: A Machine Learning Attack on LWE with Binary Secrets](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623076)
  - **Research question:** How does “SalsaPicante: A Machine Learning Attack on LWE with Binary Secrets” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors present PICANTE, an enhanced machine learning attack on LWE with sparse binary secrets, which recovers secrets in much larger dimensions (up to n=350) and with larger Hamming weights (roughly n/10, and up to h=60 for n=350).
- [ProvG-Searcher: A Graph Representation Learning Approach for Efficient Provenance Graph Search](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623187)
  - **Research question:** How does “ProvG-Searcher: A Graph Representation Learning Approach for Efficient Provenance Graph Search” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors formulate provenance search as subgraph matching and combine order embeddings with graph partitioning and behavior-preserving reduction to detect known APT behavior efficiently.
- [Privacy Leakage via Speech-induced Vibrations on Room Objects through Remote Sensing based on Phased-MIMO](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616634)
  - **Research question:** How does “Privacy Leakage via Speech-induced Vibrations on Room Objects through Remote Sensing based on Phased-MIMO” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors revisit this important line of research and show that a remote, long-distance, and even thru-the-wall speech eavesdropping attack is possible.

## 7. Other AI Security and Privacy

- ["Get in Researchers; We're Measuring Reproducibility": A Reproducibility Study of Machine Learning Papers in Tier 1 Security Conferences](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623130)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “"Get in Researchers; We're Measuring Reproducibility": A Reproducibility Study of Machine Learning Papers in Tier 1 Security Conferences” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct the first computational-reproducibility measurement of nearly 750 ML-security papers and their codebases and datasets across top security conferences from 2013 to 2022.
- [Control, Confidentiality, and the Right to be Forgotten](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3616585)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Control, Confidentiality, and the Right to be Forgotten” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that publishing a sequence of updated models that are differentially private under continual release satisfies deletion-as-control.
- [Stateful Defenses for Machine Learning Models Are Not Yet Secure Against Black-box Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623116)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Stateful Defenses for Machine Learning Models Are Not Yet Secure Against Black-box Attacks” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors show that SDMs are highly vulnerable to a new class of adaptive black-box attacks.
