# CCS 2024

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [CCS 2024 official paper list or proceedings](https://www.sigsac.org/ccs/CCS2024/program/accepted-papers.html) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [PLeak: Prompt Leaking Attacks against Large Language Model Applications](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670370)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “PLeak: Prompt Leaking Attacks against Large Language Model Applications” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors design a novel, closed-box prompt leaking attack framework, called PLeak, to optimize an adversarial query such that when the attacker sends it to a target LLM application, its response reveals its own system prompt.
- [``Do Anything Now'': Characterizing and Evaluating In-The-Wild Jailbreak Prompts on Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670388)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “``Do Anything Now'': Characterizing and Evaluating In-The-Wild Jailbreak Prompts on Large Language Models” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Employing their new framework JailbreakHub, the authors conduct a comprehensive analysis of 1,405 jailbreak prompts spanning from December 2022 to December 2023.
- [ProphetFuzz: Fully Automated Prediction and Fuzzing of High-Risk Option Combinations with Only Documentation via Large Language Model](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690231)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “ProphetFuzz: Fully Automated Prediction and Fuzzing of High-Risk Option Combinations with Only Documentation via Large Language Model” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors developed a tool called ProphetFuzz and evaluated it on a dataset comprising 52 programs collected from three related studies.
- [Optimization-based Prompt Injection Attack to LLM-as-a-Judge](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690291)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Optimization-based Prompt Injection Attack to LLM-as-a-Judge” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose JudgeDeceiver, an optimization-based prompt injection attack to LLM-as-a-Judge.
- [PromSec: Prompt Optimization for Secure Generation of Functional Source Code with Large Language Models (LLMs)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690298)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “PromSec: Prompt Optimization for Secure Generation of Functional Source Code with Large Language Models (LLMs)” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Introducing a new contrastive learning approach in gGAN, the authors formulate the code-clearing and generation loop as a dual-objective optimization problem, enabling PromSec to notably reduce the number of LLM inferences.
- [Demystifying RCE Vulnerabilities in LLM-Integrated Apps](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690338)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Demystifying RCE Vulnerabilities in LLM-Integrated Apps” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose LLMSmith to detect, validate and exploit the RCE vulnerabilities in LLM-integrated frameworks and apps.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Zero-Query Adversarial Attack on Black-box Automatic Speech Recognition Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670309)
  - **Research question:** How does “Zero-Query Adversarial Attack on Black-box Automatic Speech Recognition Systems” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose ZQ-Attack, a transfer-based adversarial attack on ASR systems in the zero-query black-box setting.
- [Watch Out! Simple Horizontal Class Backdoor Can Trivially Evade Defense](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670361)
  - **Research question:** How does “Watch Out! Simple Horizontal Class Backdoor Can Trivially Evade Defense” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a new, simple, and general type of backdoor attack, the horizontal class backdoor (HCB), that trivially breaches the class dependence characteristic of the VCB, bringing a fresh perspective to the field.
- [The Invisible Polyjuice Potion: an Effective Physical Adversarial Attack against Face Recognition](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670382)
  - **Research question:** How does “The Invisible Polyjuice Potion: an Effective Physical Adversarial Attack against Face Recognition” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel physical adversarial attack against deep face recognition systems, namely Agile (Adversarial Glasses with Infrared LasEr).
- [Distributed Backdoor Attacks on Federated Graph Learning and Certified Defenses](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690187)
  - **Research question:** How does “Distributed Backdoor Attacks on Federated Graph Learning and Certified Defenses” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** First, the authors propose an effective, stealthy, and persistent backdoor attack on FedGL.
- [Fisher Information guided Purification against Backdoor Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690250)
  - **Research question:** How does “Fisher Information guided Purification against Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Hence, to effectively obtain such re-optimization, inspired by their novel perspective establishing the connection between backdoor removal and loss smoothness, the authors propose Fisher Information guided Purification (FIP), a novel backdoor purification framework.
- [BadMerging: Backdoor Attacks Against Model Merging](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690284)
  - **Research question:** How does “BadMerging: Backdoor Attacks Against Model Merging” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Finally, the authors show that prior defense mechanisms fail to defend against their attacks, highlighting the need for more advanced defense.
- [Mithridates: Auditing and Boosting Backdoor Resistance of Machine Learning Pipelines](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690337)
  - **Research question:** How does “Mithridates: Auditing and Boosting Backdoor Resistance of Machine Learning Pipelines” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** First, the authors propose a universal, attack-agnostic resistance metric based on the minimum number of training inputs that must be compromised before the model learns any backdoor.
- [Manipulating OpenFlow Link Discovery Packet Forwarding for Topology Poisoning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690345)
  - **Research question:** How does “Manipulating OpenFlow Link Discovery Packet Forwarding for Topology Poisoning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To underscore this issue, the authors introduce Marionette, a new topology poisoning technique that manipulates OpenFlow link discovery packet forwarding to alter topology information.
- [Phantom: Untargeted Poisoning Attacks on Semi-Supervised Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690369)
  - **Research question:** How does “Phantom: Untargeted Poisoning Attacks on Semi-Supervised Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce Phantom, the first untargeted poisoning attack in SSL that disrupts the training process by injecting a small number of manipulated images into the unlabeled dataset.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [Beowulf: Mitigating Model Extraction Attacks Via Reshaping Decision Regions](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670267)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Beowulf: Mitigating Model Extraction Attacks Via Reshaping Decision Regions” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel model extraction defense, dubbed Beowulf 1, which draws inspiration from theoretical findings that models with complex and narrow decision regions are difficult to be reproduced.
- [Formal Privacy Proof of Heuristic Obfuscation – The Possibility and Impossibility of Learnable Encryption](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670277)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Formal Privacy Proof of Heuristic Obfuscation – The Possibility and Impossibility of Learnable Encryption” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present both generic possibility and impossibility results pertaining to learnable obfuscation.
- [SafeEar: Content Privacy-Preserving Audio Deepfake Detection](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670285)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “SafeEar: Content Privacy-Preserving Audio Deepfake Detection” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose SafeEar, a novel framework that aims to detect deepfake audios without relying on accessing the speech content within.
- [Data Poisoning Attacks to Locally Differentially Private Frequent Itemset Mining Protocols](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670298)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Data Poisoning Attacks to Locally Differentially Private Frequent Itemset Mining Protocols” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct extensive experiments on three datasets to compare the proposed attack with four baseline attacks.
- [Cross-silo Federated Learning with Record-level Personalized Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670351)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Cross-silo Federated Learning with Record-level Personalized Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors devise a novel framework named rPDP-FL, employing a two-stage hybrid sampling scheme with both uniform client-level sampling and non-uniform record-level sampling to accommodate varying privacy requirements.
- [ERASER: Machine Unlearning in MLaaS via an Inference Serving-Aware Approach](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670398)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “ERASER: Machine Unlearning in MLaaS via an Inference Serving-Aware Approach” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose the ERASER framework for machinE unleaRning in MLaAS via an inferencE seRving-aware approach.
- [$S^2$NeRF: Privacy-preserving Training Framework for NeRF](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690185)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “$S^2$NeRF: Privacy-preserving Training Framework for NeRF” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this concern, the authors first propose SplitNeRF, a training framework that incorporates split learning (SL) techniques to enable privacy-preserving collaborative model training between clients and servers without sharing local data.
- [Evaluations of Machine Learning Privacy Defenses are Misleading](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690194)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Evaluations of Machine Learning Privacy Defenses are Misleading” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors identify severe pitfalls in existing empirical privacy evaluations (based on membership inference attacks) that result in misleading conclusions.
- [Camel: Communication-Efficient and Maliciously Secure Federated Learning in the Shuffle Model of Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690200)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Camel: Communication-Efficient and Maliciously Secure Federated Learning in the Shuffle Model of Differential Privacy” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Following this trend, in this paper, the authors present Camel, a new communication-efficient and maliciously secure FL framework in the shuffle model of DP.
- [A Unified Membership Inference Method for Visual Self-supervised Encoder via Part-aware Capability](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690202)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “A Unified Membership Inference Method for Visual Self-supervised Encoder via Part-aware Capability” investigate?
  - **What they did:** In this setting, considering that self-supervised model could be trained by completely different self-supervised paradigms, e.g., masked image modeling and contrastive learning, with complex training details, the authors propose a unified membership inference method called PartCrop.
- [DeepCache: Revisiting Cache Side-Channel Attacks in Deep Neural Networks Executables](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690241)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “DeepCache: Revisiting Cache Side-Channel Attacks in Deep Neural Networks Executables” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Since previous works already demonstrate the abuse of cache side channels to steal DNN architectures from DL frameworks (e.g., PyTorch and TensorFlow), the authors first study using those known side-channel attacks against DNN executables.
- [Membership Inference Attacks against Vision Transformers: Mosaic MixUp Training to the Defense](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690268)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Membership Inference Attacks against Vision Transformers: Mosaic MixUp Training to the Defense” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors evaluate RAMIA on the standard ViT architecture proposed by Google (ICLR 2021), achieving high accuracy, precision, and recall performance.
- [Querycheetah: Fast Automated Discovery of Attribute Inference Attacks Against Query-Based Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690272)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Querycheetah: Fast Automated Discovery of Attribute Inference Attacks Against Query-Based Systems” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors here propose QueryCheetah, a fast and effective method for automated discovery of privacy attacks against QBSs.
- [PreCurious: How Innocent Pre-Trained Language Models Turn into Privacy Traps](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690279)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “PreCurious: How Innocent Pre-Trained Language Models Turn into Privacy Traps” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose PreCurious framework to reveal the new attack surface where the attacker releases the pre-trained model and gets a black-box access to the final fine-tuned model.
- [Uncovering Gradient Inversion Risks in Practical Language Model Training](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690292)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Uncovering Gradient Inversion Risks in Practical Language Model Training” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a domain-specific gradient inversion attack named GRAB (gradient inversion with hybrid optimization).
- [Analyzing Inference Privacy Risks Through Gradients In Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690304)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Analyzing Inference Privacy Risks Through Gradients In Machine Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a unified game-based framework that encompasses a broad range of attacks including attribute, property, distributional, and user disclosures.
- [Membership Inference Attacks Against In-Context Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690306)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Membership Inference Attacks Against In-Context Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present the first membership inference attack tailored for ICL, relying solely on generated texts without their associated probabilities.
- [Is Difficulty Calibration All We Need? Towards More Practical Membership Inference Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690316)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Is Difficulty Calibration All We Need? Towards More Practical Membership Inference Attacks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors further identify that these errors stem from an imperfect sampling of the potential distribution and a strong dependence of membership scores on the model parameters.
- [The Janus Interface: How Fine-Tuning in Large Language Models Amplifies the Privacy Risks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690325)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “The Janus Interface: How Fine-Tuning in Large Language Models Amplifies the Privacy Risks” investigate?
  - **What they did:** In their research, the authors propose a novel attack, Janus, which exploits the fine-tuning interface to recover forgotten PIIs from the pre-training data in LLMs.
- [SeqMIA: Sequential-Metric Based Membership Inference Attack](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690335)

  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a novel encoding method called Coefficients-in-Slot (CinS) encoding, which enables multiple convolutions in one HE multiplication without costly slot permutations.

## 4. Secure and Verifiable AI Systems

- [Two-Tier Data Packing in RLWE-based Homomorphic Encryption for Secure Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690191)
  - **Research question:** How does “Two-Tier Data Packing in RLWE-based Homomorphic Encryption for Secure Federated Learning” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel two-tier data encoding approach tailored for RLWE-based HE, effectively utilizing RLWE's polynomial structure.
- [Rhombus: Fast Homomorphic Matrix-Vector Multiplication for Secure Two-Party Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690281)
  - **Research question:** How does “Rhombus: Fast Homomorphic Matrix-Vector Multiplication for Secure Two-Party Inference” make AI training or inference more secure, private, or verifiable?
  - **What they did:** The authors present Rhombus, a new secure matrix-vector multiplication (MVM) protocol in the semi-honest two-party setting, which is able to be seamlessly integrated into existing privacy-preserving machine learning (PPML) frameworks and serve as the basis of secure computation in linear layers.

## 5. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [MiniCAT: Understanding and Detecting Cross-Page Request Forgery Vulnerabilities in Mini-Programs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670294)
  - **Research question:** How does “MiniCAT: Understanding and Detecting Cross-Page Request Forgery Vulnerabilities in Mini-Programs” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** To evaluate the impacts of MiniCPRF, the authors designed an automated analysis framework called MiniCAT.
- [Organic or Diffused: Can We Distinguish Human Art from AI-generated Images?](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670306)
  - **Research question:** How does “Organic or Diffused: Can We Distinguish Human Art from AI-generated Images” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** A failure to address this problem allows bad actors to defraud individuals paying a premium for human art and companies whose stated policies forbid AI imagery.
- ["Better Be Computer or I'm Dumb": A Large-Scale Evaluation of Humans as Audio Deepfake Detectors](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670325)
  - **Research question:** How does “"Better Be Computer or I'm Dumb": A Large-Scale Evaluation of Humans as Audio Deepfake Detectors” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors perform the first large-scale user study on deepfake detection.
- [MGTBench: Benchmarking Machine-Generated Text Detection](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670344)
  - **Research question:** How does “MGTBench: Benchmarking Machine-Generated Text Detection” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors fill this gap by proposing the first benchmark framework for MGT detection against powerful LLMs, named MGTBench.
- [TREC: APT Tactic / Technique Recognition via Few-Shot Provenance Subgraph Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690221)
  - **Research question:** How does “TREC: APT Tactic / Technique Recognition via Few-Shot Provenance Subgraph Learning” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose TREC, the first attempt to recognize APT tactics / techniques from provenance graphs by exploiting deep learning techniques.
- [Trident of Poseidon: A Generalized Approach for Detecting Deepfake Voices](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690311)
  - **Research question:** How does “Trident of Poseidon: A Generalized Approach for Detecting Deepfake Voices” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** In this research, the authors propose the Trident of Poseidon - a novel set of triad training strategies aimed at enhancing the generalizability of deepfake voice detection models.
- [TabularMark: Watermarking Tabular Datasets for Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690373)
  - **Research question:** How does “TabularMark: Watermarking Tabular Datasets for Machine Learning” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a hypothesis testing-based watermarking scheme, TabularMark.

## 6. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [Rules Refine the Riddle: Global Explanation for Deep Learning-Based Anomaly Detection in Security Applications](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3670375)
  - **Research question:** How does “Rules Refine the Riddle: Global Explanation for Deep Learning-Based Anomaly Detection in Security Applications” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors address the gap in the existing research by proposing GEAD , a novel global explanation for DL-based anomaly detection, to extract high-fidelity rules from DL models.

## 7. Other AI Security and Privacy

- [Pulsar: Secure Steganography for Diffusion Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690218)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Pulsar: Secure Steganography for Diffusion Models” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study securely embedding steganographic messages into the output of image diffusion models.
