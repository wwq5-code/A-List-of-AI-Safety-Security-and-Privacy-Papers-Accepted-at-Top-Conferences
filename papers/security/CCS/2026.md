# CCS 2026

This list focuses on papers about the security, privacy, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems themselves. Papers were selected by title from both the First Cycle and Second Cycle of the [official CCS 2026 accepted-paper list](https://www.sigsac.org/ccs/CCS2026/program/accepted-papers.html).

Each summary below is based on the paper's publicly available abstract. When no public abstract could be located, the entry is explicitly marked as unavailable rather than inferred from the title alone.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [DRIP: Defending Prompt Injection via Token-wise Representation Editing and Residual Fusion](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832577)
  - **Research question:** How can LLMs resist adaptive prompt injection when untrusted data contains instruction-like text without sacrificing normal task utility?
  - **What they did:** The authors trained a token-wise representation editor to remove directive semantics from data and added a residual path that keeps generation anchored to the trusted instruction.
- [Token Buncher: Shielding LLMs from Harmful Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832593)
  - **Research question:** How can a released LLM remain safe when an adversary uses reinforcement learning, rather than supervised fine-tuning, to develop harmful capabilities?
  - **What they did:** TokenBuncher constrains response entropy through entropy-as-reward RL and a Token Noiser, depriving malicious RL fine-tuning of useful reward distinctions while preserving benign fine-tunability.
- [DisarmRAG: Stealthy Retriever Poisoning to Disable Self-Correction in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832598)
  - **Research question:** Can an attacker poison a RAG retriever so that retrieved instructions suppress an LLM's self-correction and force attacker-chosen answers?
  - **What they did:** DisarmRAG co-optimizes anti-self-correction instructions and uses contrastive model editing to make a compromised retriever deliver them stealthily for targeted queries.
- [MMAligner: Safeguarding Multimodal Large Language Models through Representation Calibration](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832599)
  - **Research question:** Why do multimodal inputs bypass safety behavior learned from text, and can that gap be repaired without materially reducing model utility?
  - **What they did:** The authors identify a modality-induced representation shift and train MMAligner to move unsafe multimodal representations inside the model's existing refusal boundary while preserving benign representations.
- [Understanding and Mitigating Prompt Leaking Attacks in Real-World LLM-Based Applications](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832600)
  - **Research question:** How prevalent is system-prompt leakage in deployed LLM applications, what causes it, and how can it be mitigated without harming usability?
  - **What they did:** The study audits 1,200 applications, traces leakage to attention drift, and introduces AREA, an optimized soft prompt that re-anchors attention to defensive constraints.
- [Can Small Language Models Reliably Resist Jailbreak Attacks?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06519)
  - **Research question:** How vulnerable are small language models to jailbreak attacks, and which model or training factors determine their robustness?
  - **What they did:** The authors systematically test 59 SLMs from 15 families against 12 jailbreak methods, analyze correlates of vulnerability, and evaluate five prompt- and model-level defenses.
- [When Grammar Guides the Attack: Uncovering Control-Plane Vulnerabilities in LLMs with Structured Output](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832624)
  - **Research question:** Can grammar-guided structured-output decoding itself become a control-plane channel for bypassing an LLM's safety alignment?
  - **What they did:** The paper introduces Constrained Decoding Attack, instantiates it as EnumAttack and DictAttack, and evaluates the attacks across 13 models and five safety benchmarks.
- [MIRAGE: Misleading Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Black-box and Query-agnostic Poisoning Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832632)
  - **Research question:** Can a corpus-poisoning attack mislead RAG systems without knowing the retriever, generator, or future user queries?
  - **What they did:** MIRAGE combines persona-based query synthesis, semantic anchoring, and adversarial test-time preference optimization using only surrogate-model feedback.
- [ReasoningBomb: A Stealthy Denial-of-Service Attack by Inducing Pathologically Long Reasoning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832659)
  - **Research question:** Can short, natural-looking prompts reliably force reasoning models to consume disproportionate inference resources while evading input and output detectors?
  - **What they did:** The authors train an attacker with supervised fine-tuning and GRPO, using a hidden-state surrogate reward to optimize prompts for long, diverse reasoning traces.
- [Understanding and Preserving Safety in Fine-Tuned LLMs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832677)
  - **Research question:** How can downstream fine-tuning preserve an LLM's safety alignment without sacrificing convergence or task performance?
  - **What they did:** The paper analyzes the geometry of safety and utility gradients and proposes SPF, which projects away utility-gradient components that conflict with the low-rank safety subspace.
- [One Leak Away: How Pretrained Model Exposure Amplifies Jailbreak Risks in Finetuned LLMs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832745)
  - **Research question:** Do jailbreak prompts developed against a public pretrained model transfer to proprietary models fine-tuned from it?
  - **What they did:** The authors measure pretrain-to-finetune transfer, identify linearly separable transfer signals in hidden states, and use them for the PGP attack and a lightweight defense.
- [LocalAlign: Enabling Generalizable Prompt Injection Defense](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846553)
  - **Research question:** How can prompt-injection defenses generalize to subtle attacks whose incorrect responses remain close to the intended response?
  - **What they did:** LocalAlign generates near-but-wrong adversarial responses and applies margin-aware alignment that weights the most difficult examples more heavily during training.
- [Multimodal Resource-Exhaustion Attacks on Vision-Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846595)
  - **Research question:** Can jointly manipulating image pixels and visible prompts amplify the latency and energy cost of vision-language model inference?
  - **What they did:** The authors develop Joint Pixel-Prompt Optimization, a stagewise attack that coordinates bounded image perturbations with adversarial prompt changes across multiple VLM families.
- [A Large-scale Measurement of In-Page Prompt Injections Against LLM Web Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846661)
  - **Research question:** How common are in-page prompt injections on the public web, what purposes do they serve, and how effective are they against web agents?
  - **What they did:** The study scans 1.2 billion URLs, validates 15,300 injections, categorizes their delivery and intent, and tests them in 5,200 experiments across 13 models.
- [InceptionRAG: Stealthy Poisoning Attack Against Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846682)
  - **Research question:** Can RAG poisoning evade document-level defenses by distributing a malicious logical payload across individually benign passages?
  - **What they did:** InceptionRAG fragments misinformation into dormant multi-hop passages, optimizes authoritative suffixes with zeroth-order search, and proposes document isolation as a defense.
- [Jailbreaking via Activation Dilution](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846742)
  - **Research question:** How can decomposition-based jailbreaks learn effective sub-questions and select the right granularity while minimizing safety-relevant activation signals?
  - **What they did:** The framework trains a decomposer with online reward optimization and ranks candidate decompositions using a neuron-derived risk score from a reference model.
- [Thinking Dangerously: How Reasoning Training Dilutes Safety in Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846783)
  - **Research question:** Does safety alignment remain effective throughout long reasoning chains, or does refusal behavior erode as benign context grows?
  - **What they did:** The authors quantify safety dilution across six models, introduce premise and reasoning-chain attacks, and build ChainGuard to monitor chain length, premises, and continuation consistency.
- [Robustness of Vision Language Models Against Split-Image Harmful Input Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846760)
  - **Research question:** Can harmful semantics distributed across multiple image fragments bypass safety alignment trained primarily on holistic images?
  - **What they did:** The paper develops SIVA attacks from simple splitting to an adversarial-knowledge-distillation method that improves black-box transfer, then evaluates lightweight mitigations.
- [SEAL: Reinforcing Global Safety in Mixture-of-Experts](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846702)
  - **Research question:** Can the always-active shared experts in hybrid mixture-of-experts models provide a routing-independent anchor for safety?
  - **What they did:** SEAL attaches a parameter-efficient safety adapter to shared experts, while SEAL++ adds an orthogonal constraint to preserve existing safety subspaces under diverse attacks.
- [Understanding the Security Boundary of Obfuscation-based On-Device LLM Protection](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846712)
  - **Research question:** What common security boundary underlies obfuscation-based TEE partitioning schemes for on-device LLMs, and how can it be systematically extended?
  - **What they did:** The authors formalize composable obfuscation primitives, break prior schemes with the Collapse attack, and introduce new primitives that reduce extraction to black-box effectiveness.

## 2. AI Agent and Computer-Use Agent Security

- [Mind the Gap: Action Rebinding Attacks against Android GUI Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832590)
  - **Research question:** Can a permissionless Android app exploit the delay between a GUI agent's observation and action to redirect privileged operations into another app?
  - **What they did:** The attack swaps a benign contextual carrier for a sensitive foreground app during reasoning latency, then uses recovery loops and intent alignment for multi-step exploitation.
- [MirrorGuard: Toward Secure Computer-Use Agents via Simulation-to-Real Reasoning Correction](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832704)
  - **Research question:** Can computer-use agents learn to correct unsafe reasoning without executing dangerous actions during defense training or unnecessarily aborting benign tasks?
  - **What they did:** MirrorGuard generates high-risk GUI trajectories in a neural-symbolic text simulator and trains a plug-in defense to intercept and repair unsafe reasoning before real actions execute.
- [SkillScope: Toward Fine-Grained Least-Privilege Enforcement for Agent Skills](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846588)
  - **Research question:** How can an agent enforce least privilege when the legitimacy of a skill's action depends on the user's current task?
  - **What they did:** SkillScope builds instruction- and code-level action graphs, validates suspected over-privilege under generated tasks, and constrains confirmed actions through control flow.
- [Safeguarding LLM Agents against Long-Horizon Threats via Shadow Memory](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846601)
  - **Research question:** How can an LLM agent detect attacks whose malicious objective emerges only across a long sequence of otherwise plausible interactions?
  - **What they did:** ShadowMem maintains a separate safety-focused memory of critical trajectory context and consults it to assess the risk of each pending action before execution.
- [Governing AI Agent with Capability and Delegation Chain](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846604)
  - **Research question:** How can autonomous agents preserve fine-grained, verifiable authority across multi-hop tool and agent delegation?
  - **What they did:** CapAgent encodes API and parameter constraints in cryptographically protected, derivable credentials and provides issuance, delegation, verification, provenance, auditing, and MCP integration.
- [MemPoison: Bypassing Selective Memory Mechanisms to Plant Backdoors in LLM Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846674)
  - **Research question:** Can attackers plant persistent, triggerable backdoors despite the extraction and rewriting stages used by modern agent memory systems?
  - **What they did:** MemPoison combines a trigger-payload semantic bridge, entity-like triggers, and joint embedding optimization so malicious content survives selection, rewriting, and later retrieval.
- [Comment and Control: Hijacking Agentic Workflows via Context-Grounded Evolution](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846775)
  - **Research question:** Can attacker-controlled comments or inputs hijack LLM agents embedded in real automation workflows and reach high-impact capabilities?
  - **What they did:** JAW extracts feasible paths, prompt provenance, and runtime capabilities through hybrid program analysis, then evolves context-aware inputs that hijack GitHub Actions and n8n workflows.
- [MetaMancer: Practical Tool Selection Attacks against LLM Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846793)
  - **Research question:** Can an attacker manipulate complete tool metadata to steer black-box LLM agents toward a malicious tool while remaining effective, stealthy, and robust?
  - **What they did:** MetaMancer uses multi-agent semantic bilevel optimization and atomic metadata updates, while the proposed CVR defense reconstructs metadata around each tool's core capabilities.
- [PriMobiBench: Characterizing Visual Privacy Leakage in VLM-Driven Mobile GUI Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846776)
  - **Research question:** How much explicit private information and implicit user profiling can VLM-based mobile agents extract from screenshot trajectories?
  - **What they did:** The authors create PriMobiBench and the MobiLeak trace dataset, evaluate direct leakage and profiling, and test masking of task-irrelevant sensitive UI elements.

## 3. Model Backdoors, Poisoning, and Supply-Chain Security

- [Implementation Bugs as Attacks: Adversarial Neuron Fuzzing and Supply-Chain Backdoors](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832573)
  - **Research question:** A public abstract was not available at the time of review.
  - **What they did:** A public abstract was not available; the linked ACM record and NeuGo-A artifact confirm the paper, but no abstract-based summary is provided here.
- [Customization under Fire: Plugin Poisoning in Text-to-Image Ecosystem](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832588)
  - **Research question:** Can malicious LoRA plugins compromise text-to-image systems and propagate through normal model sharing, merging, and remixing?
  - **What they did:** PoisonLoRA implements concept hijacking and trigger-based task injection, then measures the payload's stealth, cross-model transfer, and persistence through repeated plugin merges.
- [Rethinking Backdoor Adversarial Unlearning through the Lens of Catastrophic Forgetting](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832691)
  - **Research question:** What conditions are required to completely remove backdoors rather than merely suppress their visible behavior?
  - **What they did:** The authors model backdoor learning and unlearning as continual learning and develop BI-BAU, an EM-based bilevel blind-inversion method that generates adversarial unlearning examples.
- [BadTV: Unveiling Backdoor Threats in Third-Party Task Vectors](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832640)
  - **Research question:** Can a malicious third-party task vector retain a backdoor through task learning, forgetting, and analogy arithmetic?
  - **What they did:** BadTV constructs asymmetric composite backdoors designed to survive multiple task-vector operations and evaluates them against current detection and mitigation defenses.
- [SymSan: Mitigating Malware Embedding in Large Language Models via Parameter Space Symmetry](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846529)
  - **Research question:** Can malware embedded in LLM parameters be destroyed with a defense that generalizes across attacks and provably preserves model behavior?
  - **What they did:** SymSan applies carefully paired permutation and scaling transformations derived from parameter-space symmetries to corrupt embedded payloads while maintaining functional equivalence.
- [ArchProof: A Sound Output-Contribution Certificate for Dormant-Gate Path Backdoors in ONNX Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846590)
  - **Research question:** Can ONNX models receive a deterministic, sound quantitative certificate against a formally defined class of architectural backdoors?
  - **What they did:** ArchProof computes worst-case output-contribution bounds for additive dormant-gate paths and supplies ranking, removal, poisoning-resilience, and export-stability guarantees.
- [Detecting Data Poisoning in Code Generation LLMs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846687)
  - **Research question:** How can defenders detect vulnerability-targeted poisoning in code-generation LLMs using only black-box access despite syntactic diversity in generated code?
  - **What they did:** CodeScan normalizes generations with ASTs, isolates recurring structures through iterative divergence analysis, and applies LLM-based vulnerability analysis to flag compromised models.
- [Your Space is My Zone: Security Risks of AI-Powered Applications on Pre-Trained Model Hubs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832746)
  - **Research question:** What application- and platform-level vulnerabilities arise when untrusted AI applications are hosted alongside pretrained models on public hubs?
  - **What they did:** The authors define ten attack vectors, build the Insightor analysis framework, and scan more than 970,000 public applications for access-control, validation, isolation, and leakage failures.
- [Don’t Trust the AI Ecosystem: Analyzing Privacy Leakage in Compromised Open-Source Components](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832679)
  - **Research question:** Can a compromised training component encode private examples directly into model parameters for later high-fidelity extraction?
  - **What they did:** GradLock creates deterministic parameter vaults and locks their gradients during training, enabling rapid reconstruction while resisting quantization, pruning, and fine-tuning.
- [Your Synthetic Image Is a Confession: Trojaning Image-to-Image Generative Models for Private Input Reconstruction](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846606)
  - **Research question:** Can a trojaned image-to-image model covertly encode a user's private input in an apparently normal generated output?
  - **What they did:** PIRecon compromises the diffusion pipeline's VAE to embed an input bitstream into each output and trains an attacker decoder to reconstruct the original image.
- [(A)iSpy: Parasitic Trojans for Machine Learning Infrastructure](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846713)
  - **Research question:** What attacks become possible when a malicious ML runtime component can observe and modify live tensors during training and inference?
  - **What they did:** The authors implant an ONNX Runtime trojan that exfiltrates hyperparameters and amplifies weak poisoning into reliable backdoors by monitoring transient computation-graph state.
- [From Prompt to Pwn: Exploiting GPU Memory Errors During ML Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846730)
  - **Research question:** Can remote, unprivileged inputs turn real GPU memory-safety bugs in ML software into security exploits rather than mere crashes?
  - **What they did:** The paper develops write-what-where exploits for PyTorch and CuDF and builds GPU dynamic taint tracking to locate attacker-controlled, exploitable memory objects.
- [Single Medium, Multiple Perspectives: Semantic Gaps in AI Services’ Media Preprocessing Pipelines](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846786)
  - **Research question:** Can differences between human media playback and AI preprocessing make the same file convey different content to people and AI services?
  - **What they did:** KScope uses LLM-assisted specification-driven differential testing to find parsing ambiguities and demonstrate cross-service attacks against chatbots, moderation, ASR, OCR, and serving frameworks.

## 4. AI Privacy, Memorization Leakage, and Differential Privacy

- [Private Direct Preference Optimization for LLM Alignment](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832591)
  - **Research question:** How can DPO protect the sensitive relative preferences in human feedback without adding unnecessary noise to public prompts and responses?
  - **What they did:** PrivDPO formalizes preference privacy and adds calibrated, unbiased randomness only along the one-dimensional gradient axis that carries the preference signal.
- [What Does the Server See—Understanding Privacy Leakage from Large Language Models in Split Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832653)
  - **Research question:** How much client input can an untrusted server reconstruct from intermediate activations during split LLM inference?
  - **What they did:** The authors build the ActInv reconstruction attack, define a layer-wise Perturbation Amplification Factor, and design PriPert to orient noise toward greater reconstruction error.
- [Auditing Data Provenance in LLM Fine-tuning via Intrinsic Distributional Fingerprints](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832639)
  - **Research question:** Can a data owner detect unauthorized fine-tuning after training, under black-box access and adversarial paraphrasing or distillation?
  - **What they did:** DPA extracts persistent lexical-semantic distributional fingerprints through unbiased output sampling and uses statistical hypothesis testing to audit whether proprietary data was used.
- [Code-SeC: Characterizing Secret Memorization in Code LLM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846643)
  - **Research question:** What token-level decoding signals reveal memorized credentials that may never appear in a code model's final output?
  - **What they did:** Code-SeC monitors uncertainty throughout decoding and selectively adjusts token distributions to expose latent hard-coded secrets across models and credential types.
- [Secrets Everywhere: Auditing Memorization in Mobility Prediction Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832707)
  - **Research question:** To what extent do mobility predictors memorize sensitive training trajectories at different spatial and temporal granularities?
  - **What they did:** The authors audit locations, anchor pairs, and subtrajectories using user-grounded reference sets that compare training sequences with realistic alternatives.
- [From Snapshot to Snooping: Geolocation Privacy Leakage in Large Vision Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846558)
  - **Research question:** How accurately can modern LVLMs infer private geolocation from ordinary images without metadata, and where do safety and regional biases fail?
  - **What they did:** The study evaluates 19 models from nine providers on 10,620 geotagged images across more than 180 countries under zero-shot and chain-of-thought prompting.
- [Adaptive Diffusion Freezing: Privacy-preserving Diffusion Models Against Membership Inference Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846565)
  - **Research question:** Can diffusion training reduce membership leakage without the utility loss caused by existing privacy defenses?
  - **What they did:** ADF estimates memorization risk before training and freezes high-risk data-subset and diffusion-timestep pairs through an adaptive mask matrix.
- [Hitchhiking on Inherent Randomness: Diffusion-based Graph Generation under Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832628)
  - **Research question:** Can the intrinsic randomness of graph diffusion reduce the extra noise required for formal differential privacy?
  - **What they did:** Drip combines diffusion randomness with edge-DP noise in a tight privacy analysis and uses two-stage pruning to select computationally efficient parameters.
- [Fundamental Limitations of Favorable Privacy–Utility Guarantees for DP-SGD](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832629)
  - **Research question:** Under worst-case adversarial definitions, can shuffled or Poisson-sampled DP-SGD simultaneously provide strong privacy and high utility?
  - **What they did:** The paper derives hypothesis-testing trade-off bounds that impose minimum Gaussian noise levels and empirically measures the resulting accuracy degradation.
- [Rethinking the Security of DP-SGD](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846735)
  - **Research question:** Do common DP-SGD analyses and audits overstate privacy by modeling normalized gradient updates as an unnormalized subsampled Gaussian mechanism?
  - **What they did:** The authors re-derive guarantees for expected- and batch-averaged mechanisms, audit four implementations and Opacus versions, and propose corrective mitigations.
- [G-Fence: Differentially Private Graph Neural Network Training over Federated Graphs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846547)
  - **Research question:** A public abstract was not available at the time of review.
  - **What they did:** A public abstract was not available; the linked ACM record confirms the paper, but no abstract-based summary is provided here.
- [When Topology Betrays Privacy: Reconstruction Attacks on Secure Aggregation in Decentralized Federated Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846666)
  - **Research question:** Can colluding nodes recover private updates from the asymmetric local aggregates exposed by sparse decentralized learning topologies?
  - **What they did:** The authors map reconstruction to the Hidden Subset Sum Problem and combine lattice reduction with structural filtering to recover model states and training data.
- [Beyond One-Size-Fits-All: Neural Networks for Differentially Private Tabular Data Synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846700)
  - **Research question:** When do neural generators outperform statistical models for differentially private tabular synthesis, especially under dense correlations?
  - **What they did:** MargNet incorporates adaptively chosen marginals into neural training and compares utility and speed across sparsely and densely correlated datasets.
- [Janus: Privacy and Security Risks in LLM-Integrated Android Apps](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846749)
  - **Research question:** What bidirectional privacy and security risks arise as Android apps send prompts to LLMs and reuse their generated responses?
  - **What they did:** Janus statically reconstructs end-to-end request and response flows and uses multi-agent reasoning to classify secrets, outsourcing, downstream leakage, and unsafe prompt handling.
- [TWIST: Private Tenant-Dedicated LLM Serving via Joint Model-Token Transformation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846779)
  - **Research question:** Can dedicated cloud LLM serving hide tenant prompts and outputs without cryptographic-inference costs, specialized TEEs, or severe utility loss?
  - **What they did:** TWIST applies a shared structure-preserving transformation to weights and tokens, analyzes leakage with Rényi-metric DP, and implements it for dense and MoE models.
- [Logit Laundering: Evading Data-Use Auditing in Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846794)
  - **Research question:** Can a non-compliant LLM provider suppress watermark evidence at inference time while retaining the knowledge learned from unauthorized data?
  - **What they did:** Logit Laundering estimates watermark-induced statistical bias, reshapes logits to remove it, and scales suppression according to next-token confidence to preserve utility.

## 5. Private, Verifiable, and Trustworthy AI Inference

- [DeepProve: Verifiable End-to-End Large Language Model Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832622)
  - **Research question:** How can a client efficiently verify every generated token of LLM inference performed by an untrusted cloud server?
  - **What they did:** DeepProve certifies complete output sequences with sum-check and lookup arguments for all required transformer operators and distributes proof generation across machines.
- [LightShark: Actively Secure Machine-Learning Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832717)
  - **Research question:** Can Grotto-style distributed comparison functions retain their small keys while gaining active security for two-party ML inference?
  - **What they did:** LightShark builds an authenticated DCF over a correlated GGM tree and implements maliciously secure ReLU, spline, truncation, and inference for CNN and transformer models.
- [ROSETTA: Privacy-Preserving LLM Decoding via Hybrid CKKS/TFHE Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846549)
  - **Research question:** How can private LLM decoding evaluate heterogeneous nonlinear operations more efficiently under fully homomorphic encryption?
  - **What they did:** ROSETTA combines adaptive TFHE lookup tables with an automatic operator selector that assigns each nonlinearity to CKKS or TFHE to minimize latency.
- [zkAgent: Verifiable LLM Agent Execution via One-Shot Transcript Proofs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846564)
  - **Research question:** How can users verify an agent's full autoregressive inference and external tool interactions when both are hidden behind provider APIs?
  - **What they did:** zkAgent proves an entire causal transcript in one forward pass and binds tool observations to zkTLS or zkVM subproofs in a single end-to-end SNARK.
- [zkTransformer: Scalable Zero-Knowledge Proofs for LLM Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846654)
  - **Research question:** Can zero-knowledge transformer inference become easier to compile and faster to prove without exposing model parameters?
  - **What they did:** zkTransformer compiles Einsum tensor operations into sum-check instances and uses structured exponentiation lookups to reduce nonlinear-operation proof costs.
- [PAPER: Privacy-Preserving Convolutional Neural Networks using FHE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22857)
  - **Research question:** Can deep CNN inference under leveled FHE avoid costly bootstrapping while matching plaintext accuracy and improving latency?
  - **What they did:** PAPER combines quadratic ReLU approximations, penalty-based training, structural level reduction, parameter clustering, data-layout optimization, and model ensembles.
- [PackCNN: Privacy-Preserving Convolutional Neural Network Inference](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846737)
  - **Research question:** How can ciphertext packing better exploit baby-step giant-step rotations for fast FHE-based convolutional inference?
  - **What they did:** PackCNN aligns channel rotations, co-packs multiple images, and adaptively switches between block-first and channel-first layouts for each network layer.
- [TENNOR: Trustworthy Execution for Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846750)
  - **Research question:** Can sparse neural-network training inside TEEs hide value-dependent memory access while retaining the efficiency of adaptive sparsification?
  - **What they did:** TENNOR turns sparse activation into doubly oblivious LSH retrieval and introduces multi-probe winner-take-all hashing to reduce secure index memory.
- [MOSAIC: Masked Outsourcing of Secure AI Computations](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846769)
  - **Research question:** How can a weak trusted client outsource large AI computations without revealing either its model or its input to the server?
  - **What they did:** MOSAIC masks matrix multiplications under LWE/LPN assumptions and uses controlled noise plus random Hadamard rotations to bound error across transformer layers.
- [DeepIvy: Secure Model Inference via CPU-Side TEEs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846808)
  - **Research question:** Can CPU-side TEEs protect model intellectual property while approaching native accelerator inference speed?
  - **What they did:** DeepIvy keeps a searched private adapter inside the enclave, runs a frozen public model on the accelerator, and uses one-way features and zero-copy transfer.
- [Mitigating Ciphertext Side-Channel Attacks against TEE-Shielded Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846817)
  - **Research question:** How can TEE-protected neural inference suppress ciphertext side-channel leakage without protecting every write and incurring excessive overhead?
  - **What they did:** The authors model feature leakage, identify computations and memory locations with the greatest contribution, and selectively perturb them to disrupt local and downstream inference.
- [Tricycle: Private Transformer Inference with Tricyclic Encodings](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1200)
  - **Research question:** Can homomorphic transformer inference scale by packing matrix multiplication and multi-head attention with minimal multiplicative depth?
  - **What they did:** Tricycle introduces tricyclic encodings, combines them with BSGS and GPU CKKS optimizations, and stabilizes encrypted softmax using statistical maximum estimation.
- [Azkaban: A Zero-Knowledge Abstract Analysis for Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846819)
  - **Research question:** How can zero-knowledge neural-network certification remain sound under finite-field fixed-point arithmetic and rounding error?
  - **What they did:** Azkaban provides sound interval-analysis operators for inner products, division, and nonlinear activations and applies them to private robustness and fairness certification.

## 6. Generative Content, Deepfakes, Copyright, and Provenance

- [PhantomSeal: Proactive Deepfakes Defense with Identity/Context Protection and Forensic Tracing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832581)
  - **Research question:** Can a proactive image defense prevent face swapping while protecting both identity and context and still support forensic attribution?
  - **What they did:** PhantomSeal embeds a stealthy chosen-identity cloak that redirects face swaps toward the cloak identity and leaves features suitable for tracing manipulated content.
- [Stop My Dancing! Understanding, Detecting and Attributing Motion-Aware Deepfake Videos](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832634)
  - **Research question:** What artifacts distinguish pose-guided full-body motion deepfakes, and can they support both detection and generator attribution under realistic shifts?
  - **What they did:** The authors build a 1.5-million-frame benchmark and develop MoDA, which aligns spatial semantics with multi-scale frequency features for detection and attribution.
- [PC²: Jailbreaking Attacks on GPT-based Text-to-Image Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832687)
  - **Research question:** Can black-box prompts bypass text-to-image safety filters to generate politically sensitive depictions of public figures?
  - **What they did:** PC² replaces identities with neutral descriptions and translates fragments into geopolitically distant languages, then evaluates the attack and a layered defense on GPT image models.
- [A Single Glance Is Better Than Words: Vision-Guided Targeted Concept Erasing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832589)
  - **Research question:** A public abstract was not available at the time of review.
  - **What they did:** A public abstract was not available; the linked ACM record confirms the paper, but no abstract-based summary is provided here.
- [DSS: Dynamic Semantic Steering for Robust Concept Erasure in Diffusion Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846633)
  - **Research question:** Can a training-free diffusion defense erase risky concepts robustly without over-correcting prompts or degrading benign image generation?
  - **What they did:** DSS identifies benign semantic anchors through density-based boundaries and applies closed-form, cross-attention-guided feature corrections constrained to local semantic geometry.
- [What Concepts Lie Within? Detecting and Suppressing Risky Content in Diffusion Transformers](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846745)
  - **Research question:** Can concept-specific attention-head behavior reveal and suppress risky generation even when adversarial prompts omit explicit unsafe words?
  - **What they did:** The method derives attention-head-vector risk signatures, tracks them across denoising with momentum, and adaptively suppresses risky token attention logits at inference time.
- [DWBench: Evaluation of Watermark for Dataset Copyright Auditing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846591)
  - **Research question:** How reliably do dataset watermarks support copyright auditing under standardized, low-ratio, multi-user, and multi-watermark conditions?
  - **What they did:** DWBench organizes implementation and verification choices and evaluates 25 watermarking methods with unified sample- and dataset-level metrics across classification and generation.
- [NotaryMark: Dataset Usage Auditing in Text-to-Image Diffusion Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846603)
  - **Research question:** How can a multi-bit dataset watermark survive the noising-denoising process and enable black-box auditing of diffusion-model training?
  - **What they did:** NotaryMark aligns its signal with latent denoising, aggregates multiple queries by voting, and applies error-correcting codes for reliable identifier recovery.
- [PathMark: Protecting Mixture-of-Expert LLMs via Path Watermarks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832747)
  - **Research question:** How can watermarks remain detectable in MoE models whose dynamic routing prevents consistent activation of marked parameters?
  - **What they did:** PathMark encodes ownership by steering triggered tokens through predetermined expert paths, using margin widening, wide paths, and contrastive isolation for robustness.
- [PURA: Watermarking for AI-Generated Text Attribution](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846709)
  - **Research question:** Can multi-bit text watermarks provide high-capacity, edit-robust attribution while preserving the original generation distribution exactly?
  - **What they did:** PURA embeds bits in keyed inverse-transform sampling and recovers them from soft token-interval evidence aggregated across the generated sequence.
- [Proof-of-Authorship for Diffusion-based AI Generated Content](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846801)
  - **Research question:** Can a diffusion-image creator prove authorship more strongly than conventional timestamping or watermark-based ownership claims?
  - **What they did:** The framework cryptographically binds the generator's random seed to the author's identity and uses a probabilistic adjudicator to quantify false claims without secrets.
- [SkillCODER: Auditing Copyright Infringement in LLM Agent Skills](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846644)
  - **Research question:** How can owners audit redistribution and identify buyers of long, readable agent-skill documents through black-box access?
  - **What they did:** SkillCODER parses skills into structural anchors, embeds an artifact-gated ownership capsule, and encodes buyer fingerprints with controlled-vocabulary error-correcting codes.

## 7. Autonomous Driving, Voice, and Biometric Security

- [SlowPerception: Physical-World Latency Attack against Camera-based Perception in Autonomous Driving](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832618)
  - **Research question:** Can a physical-world attack create enough perception latency across detection and tracking to cause real autonomous-driving safety failures?
  - **What they did:** SlowPerception manipulates projected phantom-object bounding-box shapes to amplify computation across the camera pipeline and tests resulting collisions and sign violations.
- [Investigating Planning Attacks against End-to-End Autonomous Driving](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846795)
  - **Research question:** Can sensor perturbations corrupt shared latent representations and cause unsafe end-to-end driving plans while observable outputs remain deceptively normal?
  - **What they did:** JANUS jointly optimizes perturbations for planning failure and output consistency, then evaluates attacks and consistency-based detection on three driving systems.
- [From Stealthy Data Fabrication to Unsafe Driving: Attacks on Collaborative Perception](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846810)
  - **Research question:** Can small, hard-to-detect changes to shared perception data propagate into safety-critical behavior in realistic, dynamic traffic scenarios?
  - **What they did:** The attack subtly shifts object poses and adapts online to traffic conditions, exploiting downstream tracking and prediction; the authors also test localized anomaly detection.
- [Illusion of Depth: Hidden Stereo Vision Vulnerabilities in Depth Estimation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832649)
  - **Research question:** Can simple physical patterns exploit stereo sampling and calibration to control estimated obstacle depth across classical and learned systems?
  - **What they did:** The authors use repeating patterns to shift perceived depth, evaluate algorithms, models, sensor fusion, and commercial cameras, and propose similarity-based suppression.
- [PolyVoice: One-to-Many Impersonation in Speaker Verification Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832636)
  - **Research question:** Can one reusable utterance impersonate multiple unseen users by exploiting overlapping acceptance regions in clustered speaker embeddings?
  - **What they did:** PolyVoice constructs cluster-aligned samples from public speech and optimizes them across surrogate models for black-box transfer to 15 verification systems.
- [Expose Your Disguise: Recovering Source Speaker Identity From Voice Conversion](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832627)
  - **Research question:** Can forensic analysis recover the original speaker's identity from audio altered by an unknown voice-conversion system?
  - **What they did:** TRIDENT combines a source-identity extractor with auxiliary branches that identify the conversion mechanism and separate the target speaker's latent representation.
- [Tsuikami: Cross-Channel Source Speaker Tracing Under Voice Conversion](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846697)
  - **Research question:** How can source-speaker tracing remain accurate when enrollment and converted test audio travel through different telephony, mobile, or VoIP channels?
  - **What they did:** Tsuikami simulates channel effects during enrollment and jointly optimizes paired restored and enrolled voiceprints to align their embeddings across channels.
- [Casting the Net! Revisiting MasterFace Impersonation Attacks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832675)
  - **Research question:** Can realistic decision-only attackers again exceed baseline false-match rates against modern face-recognition systems using provider-specific MasterFaces?
  - **What they did:** The paper formulates impersonation as maximum coverage and builds geometric, API-tailored nets of faces that cover many acceptance regions within few trials.

## Adjacent Scope: AI for Security

The following papers use AI for vulnerability discovery, fuzzing, or attack detection rather than studying the security of AI systems themselves. They are listed separately and are not part of the core collection above.

- [CVE-Genie](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832602)
  - **Research question:** Can LLM agents automatically reconstruct vulnerable environments and verifiable exploits from public CVE records at useful scale and cost?
  - **What they did:** CVE-Genie coordinates multiple agents to gather resources, rebuild environments, and reproduce exploits, successfully producing verifiable cases for 428 of 841 CVEs.
- [From Documentation to Zero-day Vulnerabilities](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832609)
  - **Research question:** Can API documentation guide generation of meaningful multi-call test cases that expose PDF-reader vulnerabilities missed by existing fuzzers?
  - **What they did:** PDFuzzer uses an LLM to infer grammars and API relationships from manuals and traces, then uses constraint solving to generate concrete call sequences.
- [BACAgent](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832642)
  - **Research question:** Can intended access-control policies be inferred from an application's codebase well enough to detect broken-access-control vulnerabilities?
  - **What they did:** BACAgent combines LLM-RAG analysis of implemented checks with semantic code evidence, infers intended policies, and flags inconsistencies with actual enforcement.
- [PBFuzz](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832663)
  - **Research question:** Can an agent efficiently generate proof-of-vulnerability inputs by jointly reasoning about reachability and vulnerability-triggering constraints?
  - **What they did:** PBFuzz combines autonomous code reasoning, specialized analysis tools, persistent hypothesis memory, debugging feedback, and property-based testing in an agentic fuzzing loop.
- [LDBF](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846613)
  - **Research question:** How can fuzzers generate verifier-compliant eBPF programs and compatible execution contexts that thoroughly exercise the under-tested runtime?
  - **What they did:** LDBF pairs LLM-guided program synthesis and compiler-verifier feedback with deterministic, adaptive orchestration of attach points and runtime resources.
- [PrivEscalate](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846719)
  - **Research question:** How capable and robust are LLM agents at Linux privilege escalation across vulnerability classes, environmental variants, and agent architectures?
  - **What they did:** The authors build 531 Dockerized scenarios and 329 variants, evaluate six models and three agent designs, and develop the enumeration- and planning-enhanced PrivEscAgent.
- [Global-View Reasoning with LLMs for Real-World Vulnerability Detection](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846788)
  - **Research question:** How can LLMs extend strong local code reasoning to vulnerability analysis across large, real-world software projects?
  - **What they did:** SemScan iterates between evidence collection and evidence-guided semantic synthesis, then evaluates the resulting global analysis on CVEs and 200 Python projects.
- [CircumVolve](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846759)
  - **Research question:** Can LLM-guided program synthesis automatically discover censorship-evasion strategies for encrypted and complex protocols beyond grammar-based search?
  - **What they did:** CircumVolve represents evasions as executable Python and evolves them through LLM-proposed, feedback-driven mutations across TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP, TLS, and QUIC.
- [The Illusion of Rust Safety](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3832709)
  - **Research question:** Can apparently safe Rust functions still cause undefined behavior because their safety depends on hidden module-level API invariants?
  - **What they did:** The authors define five root causes for modular unsafe functions and build Coin, a fine-tuned LLM system for detecting them and generating proof-of-concept exploits.
- [IoTProber](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830454.3846755)
  - **Research question:** Can Internet-scale IoT device type and vendor identification overcome sparse fingerprints, long-tail knowledge, and LLM hallucination?
  - **What they did:** IoTProber builds a hierarchical graph of known-device relationships, retrieves related evidence at local and global levels, and grounds LLM identification and report generation.
