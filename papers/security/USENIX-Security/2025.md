# USENIX Security 2025

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [USENIX Security 2025 official paper list](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/technical-sessions) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence. Method summaries are grounded in the abstract; if no public abstract was available, the entry says so explicitly instead of inferring a contribution from the title.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [Mind the Inconspicuous: Revealing the Hidden Weakness in Aligned LLMs' Refusal Boundaries](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/yu-jiahao)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Mind the Inconspicuous: Revealing the Hidden Weakness in Aligned LLMs' Refusal Boundaries” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Building on this observation, the authors propose a straightforward method to BOOST jailbreak attacks by appending eos tokens.
- [Activation Approximations Can Incur Safety Vulnerabilities in Aligned LLMs: Comprehensive Analysis and Defense](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zhang-jiawen)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Activation Approximations Can Incur Safety Vulnerabilities in Aligned LLMs: Comprehensive Analysis and Defense” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose QuadA, a novel safety enhancement method tailored to mitigate the safety compromises introduced by activation approximations.
- [Exposing the Guardrails: Reverse-Engineering and Jailbreaking Safety Filters in DALL·E Text-to-Image Pipelines](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/villa)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Exposing the Guardrails: Reverse-Engineering and Jailbreaking Safety Filters in DALL·E Text-to-Image Pipelines” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate the specific design and implementation of safety guardrails in black-box text-to-image (T2I) models, such as DALL·E, which are implemented to prevent potential misuse from generating harmful image content.
- [On the Proactive Generation of Unsafe Images From Text-To-Image Models Using Benign Prompts](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/wu-yixin-generation)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “On the Proactive Generation of Unsafe Images From Text-To-Image Models Using Benign Prompts” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors propose a stealthy poisoning attack method that balances covertness and performance.
- [Bridging the Gap in Vision Language Models in Identifying Unsafe Concepts Across Modalities](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/qu-yiting)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Bridging the Gap in Vision Language Models in Identifying Unsafe Concepts Across Modalities” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors first compile the UnsafeConcepts dataset, featuring 75 unsafe concepts, i.e., "Swastika," "Sexual Harassment," and "Assaults," along with associated 1.5K images.
- [When LLMs Go Online: The Emerging Threat of Web-Enabled LLMs](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/kim-hanna)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “When LLMs Go Online: The Emerging Threat of Web-Enabled LLMs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors investigate the risks associated with misuse of LLM agents in cyberattacks involving personal data.
- [TwinBreak: Jailbreaking LLM Security Alignments based on Twin Prompts](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/krauss)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “TwinBreak: Jailbreaking LLM Security Alignments based on Twin Prompts” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce TwinBreak, an innovative safety alignment removal method.
- [Exploiting Task-Level Vulnerabilities: An Automatic Jailbreak Attack and Defense Benchmarking for LLMs](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zhang-lan)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Exploiting Task-Level Vulnerabilities: An Automatic Jailbreak Attack and Defense Benchmarking for LLMs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** In contrast to these prompt-level or token-level jailbreaks, the authors present a novel task-level jailbreak based on knowledge decomposition, which does not rely on any specific prompts or tokens.
- [StruQ: Defending Against Prompt Injection with Structured Queries](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/chen-sizhe)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “StruQ: Defending Against Prompt Injection with Structured Queries” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce structured queries, a general approach to tackle this problem.
- [Great, Now Write an Article About That: The Crescendo Multi-Turn LLM Jailbreak Attack](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/russinovich)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Great, Now Write an Article About That: The Crescendo Multi-Turn LLM Jailbreak Attack” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce a novel jailbreak attack called Crescendo.

  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “SelfDefend: LLMs Can Defend Themselves against Jailbreaking in a Practical Manner” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Inspired by how the traditional security concept of shadow stacks defends against memory overflow attacks, this paper introduces a generic LLM jailbreak defense framework called SelfDefend, which establishes a shadow LLM as a defense instance (in detection state)…
- [Are CAPTCHAs Still Bot-hard? Generalized Visual CAPTCHA Solving with Agentic Vision Language Model](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/teoh)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Are CAPTCHAs Still Bot-hard? Generalized Visual CAPTCHA Solving with Agentic Vision Language Model” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors build Halligan, a VLM-based generalized solver that reduces unseen visual CAPTCHA challenges to search problems, and test it on 2,600 challenges across 26 types plus a 30-day real-world CAPTCHA-farm deployment.
- [Make Agent Defeat Agent: Automatic Detection of Taint-Style Vulnerabilities in LLM-based Agents](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/liu-fengyu)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Make Agent Defeat Agent: Automatic Detection of Taint-Style Vulnerabilities in LLM-based Agents” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel directed greybox fuzzing approach, called AgentFuzz, the first fuzzing framework for detecting taint-style vulnerabilities in LLM-based agents.
- [Logs In, Patches Out: Automated Vulnerability Repair via Tree-of-Thought LLM Analysis](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/kim-youngjoon)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Logs In, Patches Out: Automated Vulnerability Repair via Tree-of-Thought LLM Analysis” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose SAN2PATCH, a system that generates patches using only sanitizer logs and source code, eliminating the need for costly program analysis or manual intervention.
- [Hybrid Language Processor Fuzzing via LLM-Based Constraint Solving](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/yang-yupeng)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Hybrid Language Processor Fuzzing via LLM-Based Constraint Solving” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To effectively address these challenges, the authors propose two novel solutions, hybrid centrality prioritization and iterative context construction.
- [EchoLLM: LLM-Augmented Acoustic Eavesdropping Attack on Bone Conduction Headphones with mmWave Radar](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/yao-xin)

  - **What they did:** The authors propose EchoLLM, the first mmWave-based eavesdropping attack specifically targeting the semantic content of audio transmitted through a victim's bone conduction headphones.
- [Low-Cost and Comprehensive Non-textual Input Fuzzing with LLM-Synthesized Input Generators](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zhang-kunpeng)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Low-Cost and Comprehensive Non-textual Input Fuzzing with LLM-Synthesized Input Generators” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel approach to enabling grammar-aware fuzzing over non-textual inputs.
- [Unsafe LLM-Based Search: Quantitative Analysis and Mitigation of Safety Risks in AI Web Search](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/luo-zeren)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Unsafe LLM-Based Search: Quantitative Analysis and Mitigation of Safety Risks in AI Web Search” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct the first safety risk quantification on seven production AIPSEs by systematically defining the threat model, risk type, and evaluating responses to various query types.
- [JBShield: Defending Large Language Models from Jailbreak Attacks through Activated Concept Analysis and Manipulation](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zhang-shenyi)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “JBShield: Defending Large Language Models from Jailbreak Attacks through Activated Concept Analysis and Manipulation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Building on their analysis, the authors propose a comprehensive jailbreak defense framework, JBShield, consisting of two key components: jailbreak detection JBShield-D and mitigation JBShield-M.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [EmbedX: Embedding-Based Cross-Trigger Backdoor Attack Against Large Language Models](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/yan-nan)
  - **Research question:** How does “EmbedX: Embedding-Based Cross-Trigger Backdoor Attack Against Large Language Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose EmbedX, an effective and efficient cross-trigger backdoor attack against LLMs.
- [Watch the Watchers! On the Security Risks of Robustness-Enhancing Diffusion Models](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/li-changjiang)
  - **Research question:** How does “Watch the Watchers! On the Security Risks of Robustness-Enhancing Diffusion Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Specifically, the authors demonstrate that these models are highly susceptible to DIFF2, a simple yet effective attack, which substantially diminishes their robustness assurance.
- [Data-Free Model-Related Attacks: Unleashing the Potential of Generative AI](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/ye-attacks)
  - **Research question:** How does “Data-Free Model-Related Attacks: Unleashing the Potential of Generative AI” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors introduce the use of generative AI for facilitating model-related attacks, including model extraction, membership inference, and model inversion.
- [SoK: Efficiency Robustness of Dynamic Deep Learning Systems](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/rathnasuriya)
  - **Research question:** How does “SoK: Efficiency Robustness of Dynamic Deep Learning Systems” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Through an in-depth evaluation, the authors analyze adversarial strategies that target DDLSs efficiency and identify key challenges in securing these systems.
- [VAPD: An Anomaly Detection Model for PDF Malware Forensics with Adversarial Robustness](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/liu-side)
  - **Research question:** How does “VAPD: An Anomaly Detection Model for PDF Malware Forensics with Adversarial Robustness” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose VAPD, an anomaly detection model based on reconstruction with dual forensics objectives: 1) identifying PDF malware through the reconstruction error between input and output, and 2) pinpointing anomalous regions.
- [Rowhammer-Based Trojan Injection: One Bit Flip Is Sufficient for Backdooring DNNs](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/li-xiang)
  - **Research question:** How does “Rowhammer-Based Trojan Injection: One Bit Flip Is Sufficient for Backdooring DNNs” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address these limitations, the authors propose ONEFLIP, the first one-bit-flip backdoor attack on full-precision models.
- [Revisiting Training-Inference Trigger Intensity in Backdoor Attacks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/lin-chenhao)
  - **Research question:** How does “Revisiting Training-Inference Trigger Intensity in Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** TITIM specifically investigates the training-inference trigger intensity, such as the size or the opacity of a trigger, and reveals new insights into trigger generalization and overfitting.
- [Persistent Backdoor Attacks in Continual Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/guo-zhen)
  - **Research question:** How does “Persistent Backdoor Attacks in Continual Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this gap, the authors introduce two persistent backdoor attacks–Blind Task Backdoor and Latent Task Backdoor–each leveraging minimal adversarial influence.
- [DeBackdoor: A Deductive Framework for Detecting Backdoor Attacks on Deep Models with Limited Data](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/popovic)
  - **Research question:** How does “DeBackdoor: A Deductive Framework for Detecting Backdoor Attacks on Deep Models with Limited Data” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present a novel framework for detecting backdoors under realistic restrictions.
- [PoiSAFL: Scalable Poisoning Attack Framework to Byzantine-resilient Semi-asynchronous Federated Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/pang-xiaoyi)
  - **Research question:** How does “PoiSAFL: Scalable Poisoning Attack Framework to Byzantine-resilient Semi-asynchronous Federated Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To plug this gap and fully explore the vulnerability of SAFL, in this paper, the authors propose a scalable stealth poisoning attack framework for Byzantine-resilient SAFL, called PoiSAFL.
