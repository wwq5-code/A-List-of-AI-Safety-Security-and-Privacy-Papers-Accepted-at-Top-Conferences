# USENIX Security 2023

This list focuses on accepted papers about the security, privacy, safety, and trustworthiness of AI/ML systems. Papers were screened by title from the [USENIX Security 2023 official paper list or proceedings](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/technical-sessions) and then checked against their publicly available abstracts.

Each `Research question` and `What they did` line is one sentence, and every method summary is grounded in the paper abstract.

## 1. LLM and Agent Security

- [Lost at C: A User Study on the Security Implications of Large Language Model Code Assistants](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/sandoval)
  - **Research question:** What LLM or agent attack surface, failure mode, or defense does “Lost at C: A User Study on the Security Implications of Large Language Model Code Assistants” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors conduct a security-driven user study (N=58) to assess code written by student programmers when assisted by LLMs.

## 2. Backdoors, Poisoning, and Adversarial ML

- [Black-box Adversarial Example Attack towards FCG Based Android Malware Detection under Incomplete Feature Information](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/li-heng)
  - **Research question:** How does “Black-box Adversarial Example Attack towards FCG Based Android Malware Detection under Incomplete Feature Information” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors design a novel black-box AE attack towards the FCG based malware detection system, called BagAmmo.
- [Evading Provenance-Based ML Detectors with Adversarial System Actions](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/mukherjee)
  - **Research question:** How does “Evading Provenance-Based ML Detectors with Adversarial System Actions” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors present PROVNINJA, a framework designed to generate adversarial attacks that aim to elude provenance-based Machine Learning (ML) security detectors.
- [Meta-Sift: How to Sift Out a Clean Subset in the Presence of Data Poisoning?](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/zeng)
  - **Research question:** How does “Meta-Sift: How to Sift Out a Clean Subset in the Presence of Data Poisoning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors analyze five representative defenses that use base sets and find that their performance deteriorates dramatically with less than 1% poisoned points in the base set.
- [Towards A Proactive ML Approach for Detecting Backdoor Poison Samples](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/qi)
  - **Research question:** How does “Towards A Proactive ML Approach for Detecting Backdoor Poison Samples” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** Third, the authors introduce the technique of Confusion Training (CT) as a concrete instantiation of their framework.
- [PORE: Provably Robust Recommender Systems against Data Poisoning Attacks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/jia)
  - **Research question:** How does “PORE: Provably Robust Recommender Systems against Data Poisoning Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To prevent such cat-and-mouse game, the authors propose PORE, the first framework to build provably robust recommender systems in this work.
- [Every Vote Counts: Ranking-Based Training of Federated Learning to Resist Poisoning Attacks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/mozaffari)
  - **Research question:** How does “Every Vote Counts: Ranking-Based Training of Federated Learning to Resist Poisoning Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To address this, the authors propose Federated Rank Learning (FRL).
- [A Data-free Backdoor Injection Approach in Neural Networks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/lv)
  - **Research question:** How does “A Data-free Backdoor Injection Approach in Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose a novel backdoor injection approach in a "data-free" manner.
- [Sparsity Brings Vulnerabilities: Exploring New Metrics in Backdoor Attacks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/tian)
  - **Research question:** How does “Sparsity Brings Vulnerabilities: Exploring New Metrics in Backdoor Attacks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To exploit this sparsity-related vulnerability, the authors propose a clean-label backdoor attack consisting of a dissimilarity metric-based candidate selection and a variation ratio-based trigger construction.%, which shows the strongest attack performance compared with previous strategies.
- [Aliasing Backdoor Attacks on Pre-trained Models](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/wei-chengan)
  - **Research question:** How does “Aliasing Backdoor Attacks on Pre-trained Models” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose the aliasing backdoor, a low-cost and data-free attack that threatens mainstream pre-trained models and transfers to all student models fine-tuned from them.
- [ASSET: Robust Backdoor Data Detection Across a Multiplicity of Deep Learning Paradigms](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/pan)
  - **Research question:** How does “ASSET: Robust Backdoor Data Detection Across a Multiplicity of Deep Learning Paradigms” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** By evaluating 56 attack settings, the authors show that the performance of most existing detection methods varies significantly across different attacks and poison ratios, and all fail on the state-of-the-art clean-label backdoor attack which only manipulates a few training data's features with imperceptible noise without changing labels.
- [VILLAIN: Backdoor Attacks Against Vertical Split Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/bai)
  - **Research question:** How does “VILLAIN: Backdoor Attacks Against Vertical Split Learning” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To tackle these challenges, the authors propose VILLAIN, a backdoor attack framework that features effective label inference and data poisoning strategies.
- [“Security is not my field, I’m a stats guy”: A Qualitative Root Cause Analysis of Barriers to Adversarial Machine Learning Defenses in Industry](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/mink)
  - **Research question:** How does ““Security is not my field, I’m a stats guy”: A Qualitative Root Cause Analysis of Barriers to Adversarial Machine Learning Defenses in Industry” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** To better understand the reasons behind the lack of deployed AML defenses, the authors conduct semi-structured interviews (n=21) with data scientists and data engineers to explore what barriers impede the effective implementation of such defenses.
- [Precise and Generalized Robustness Certification for Neural Networks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/yuan-yuanyuan-certification)
  - **Research question:** How does “Precise and Generalized Robustness Certification for Neural Networks” expose, measure, or mitigate backdoor, poisoning, or adversarial risk in AI models?
  - **What they did:** The authors formulate a comprehensive set of semantic-level image mutations uniformly as certain directions in the latent space of generative models.

## 3. AI Privacy, Memorization, and Unlearning

- [PoliGraph: Automated Privacy Policy Analysis using Knowledge Graphs](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/cui)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “PoliGraph: Automated Privacy Policy Analysis using Knowledge Graphs” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors define a knowledge-graph representation of whole privacy policies and build an NLP system that extracts the graphs for automated consistency and disclosure analysis.
- [Calpric: Inclusive and Fine-grain Labeling of Privacy Policies with Crowdsourcing and Active Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/qiu)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Calpric: Inclusive and Fine-grain Labeling of Privacy Policies with Crowdsourcing and Active Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To address these challenges, the authors present Calpric , which combines automatic text selection and segmentation, active learning and the use of crowdsourced annotators to generate a large, balanced training set for privacy policies at low cost.
- [PrivateFL: Accurate, Differentially Private Federated Learning via Personalized Data Transformation](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/yang-yuchen)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “PrivateFL: Accurate, Differentially Private Federated Learning via Personalized Data Transformation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the accuracy degradation cause of FL+DP and then design an approach to improve the accuracy.
- [Tight Auditing of Differentially Private Machine Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/nasr)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Tight Auditing of Differentially Private Machine Learning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors design an improved auditing scheme that yields tight privacy estimates for natural (not adversarially crafted) datasets—if the adversary can see all model updates during training.
- [CodexLeaks: Privacy Leaks from Code Generation Language Models in GitHub Copilot](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/niu)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “CodexLeaks: Privacy Leaks from Code Generation Language Models in GitHub Copilot” investigate?
  - **What they did:** To overcome the non-public training data, the authors propose a semi-automated filtering method using a blind membership inference attack.
- [Inductive Graph Unlearning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/wang-cheng-long)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “Inductive Graph Unlearning” investigate?
  - **What they did:** Empirically, the authors evaluate their method on several inductive benchmarks and evolving transaction graphs.
- [GAP: Differentially Private Graph Neural Networks with Aggregation Perturbation](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/sajadmanesh)
  - **Research question:** What privacy, memorization, extraction, or forgetting problem does “GAP: Differentially Private Graph Neural Networks with Aggregation Perturbation” investigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors study the problem of learning Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with Differential Privacy (DP).

## 4. Deepfakes, Watermarking, and Content Provenance

- [PTW: Pivotal Tuning Watermarking for Pre-Trained Image Generators](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/lukas)
  - **Research question:** How does “PTW: Pivotal Tuning Watermarking for Pre-Trained Image Generators” address generated-content detection, attribution, authenticity, copyright, or provenance?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose rigorous, game-based definitions for robustness and undetectability and their study reveals that watermarking is not robust against an adaptive white-box attacker who has control over the generator's parameters.

## 5. AI Safety, Alignment, and Responsible AI

- [Towards Targeted Obfuscation of Adversarial Unsafe Images using Reconstruction and Counterfactual Super Region Attribution Explainability](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/bethany)
  - **Research question:** How does “Towards Targeted Obfuscation of Adversarial Unsafe Images using Reconstruction and Counterfactual Super Region Attribution Explainability” evaluate or improve AI safety, alignment, oversight, or responsible deployment?
  - **What they did:** The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their system with a large-scale evaluation on three common unsafe images: Sexually Explicit, Cyberbullying, and Self-Harm.

## 6. AI for Cybersecurity and Abuse Detection

- [IvySyn: Automated Vulnerability Discovery in Deep Learning Frameworks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/christou)
  - **Research question:** How does “IvySyn: Automated Vulnerability Discovery in Deep Learning Frameworks” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors present IvySyn, the first fully-automated framework for discovering memory error vulnerabilities in Deep Learning (DL) frameworks.
- [The Case for Learned Provenance Graph Storage Systems](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/ding-hailun-provenance)
  - **Research question:** How does “The Case for Learned Provenance Graph Storage Systems” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors build LEONARD, which learns a DNN-based provenance-graph store and adds domain-aware redundancy reduction and indexes for compact, efficient forensic queries.
- [PROGRAPHER: An Anomaly Detection System based on Provenance Graph Embedding](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/yang-fan)
  - **Research question:** How does “PROGRAPHER: An Anomaly Detection System based on Provenance Graph Embedding” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** The authors design a new anomaly detection system on provenance graphs, termed PROGRAPHER.
- [Differential Testing of Cross Deep Learning Framework APIs: Revealing Inconsistencies and Vulnerabilities](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/deng-zizhuang)
  - **Research question:** How does “Differential Testing of Cross Deep Learning Framework APIs: Revealing Inconsistencies and Vulnerabilities” use AI to improve a conventional security or abuse-detection task?
  - **What they did:** To this end, the authors propose TENSORSCOPE, a novel approach to test cross-framework APIs for security bugs.

## 7. Other AI Security and Privacy

- [Aegis: Mitigating Targeted Bit-flip Attacks against Deep Neural Networks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/wang-jialai)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Aegis: Mitigating Targeted Bit-flip Attacks against Deep Neural Networks” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** The authors propose Aegis, a novel defense method to mitigate targeted BFAs.
- [PCAT: Functionality and Data Stealing from Split Learning by Pseudo-Client Attack](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/gao)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “PCAT: Functionality and Data Stealing from Split Learning by Pseudo-Client Attack” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** Different from the conventional model inversion, the authors investigate the inherent privacy leakage through the server model in SL and reveal that clients' functionality and private data can be easily stolen by the server model, and a series of intermediate server models during SL can even cause more leakage.
- [NeuroPots: Realtime Proactive Defense against Bit-Flip Attacks in Neural Networks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/liu-qi)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “NeuroPots: Realtime Proactive Defense against Bit-Flip Attacks in Neural Networks” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** Specifically, the authors propose a proactive defense concept named NeuroPots, which embeds a few "honey neurons” as crafted vulnerabilities into the DNN model to lure the attacker into injecting faults in them, thus making detection and model recovery efficient.
- [Gradient Obfuscation Gives a False Sense of Security in Federated Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/yue)
  - **Research question:** What AI security or privacy weakness does “Gradient Obfuscation Gives a False Sense of Security in Federated Learning” expose or mitigate?
  - **What they did:** Additionally, the authors design a new method under the proposed framework to reconstruct images at the semantic level.
